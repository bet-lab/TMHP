Air-side fan & heat exchanger¶

Air-side fan UA and power utility functions.

Airflow-based correlations for the outdoor-coil heat exchanger: a velocity-dependent UA scaling law (calc_UA_from_dV_fan) and a fan power curve (calc_fan_power_from_dV_fan). HX performance solving lives in enex_functions and HP schedule checking in dynamic_context.

tmhp.hx_fan.INDOOR_AIRFLOW_PER_W = 5.37e-05¶

Default indoor-unit airflow per watt of nameplate capacity [m3/s per W].

400 cfm per ton of refrigeration (0.18878 m3/s per 3516.85 W), the nominal airflow at which unitary air conditioners and heat pumps are rated (AHRI 210/240 uses it as the airflow when none is specified, and caps the rated airflow at 450 cfm/ton). In SI it is 193 m3/h per kW. The air-to-air units this library is validated against publish 168 to 309 m3/h per kW at their high fan speed (Daikin FTXM-A, 2.0 to 5.0 kW: 0.1717 to 0.2333 m3/s), so the figure sits at the lower end of what split systems run.

The default is the single-zone VAV surrogate with an independent 10% electrical floor. Its 15% airflow bound is a TMHP control assumption. The following fixed-SP curve remains an explicit alternative.

Air properties¶

Air properties at the state an air coil draws its air.

A fan moves a volume of air, a coil exchanges heat with a mass of it. The two are tied by the density of the air the fan draws, and that density follows the air temperature: at a fixed volume flow, air at -25 degC carries 16 % more mass (and heat capacity) than the 15 degC standard air of constants.rho_a, air at 35 degC 6.5 % less.

air_density is the ideal-gas density of dry air, rho = P / (R_a T), with R_a = 287.055 J/(kg K) (constants.R_a). At 15 degC and 101 325 Pa it gives 1.2250 kg/m3, the value of constants.rho_a, so the two agree at the standard state.

What is left out, and how much it is:

  • Humidity. Water vapour makes moist air lighter and its specific heat larger; per cubic metre the two nearly cancel. At 20 degC and 50 % relative humidity the sensible heat capacity of a cubic metre is 0.2 % above the dry-air figure, at 35 degC and 40 % it is 0.3 % above. The coils of the cycle models are dry (sensible heat only), so dry air is used.

  • Specific heat. constants.c_a (1005 J/(kg K)) is kept constant: the specific heat of dry air changes by 0.1 % between -25 and 40 degC.

  • Altitude. The pressure argument defaults to one standard atmosphere. The models use that default; at 1 600 m the density is 18 % lower.

  • Fan power. The fan curves stay functions of the volume flow at the rated static pressure. By the fan laws the pressure rise, and so the power, is proportional to the density at a fixed volume flow; the models do not apply that (outdoor fan at -25 degC: +16 % of its power, 0.5 to 0.7 % of the total electrical input).

The volume flows of the air-source models (dV_ou_a, dV_iu_a, the fan references and limits) are volumes at the coil inlet state, so the air mass flow of a coil is air_density(T_in) * dV and is the same before and after the coil and the fan.

class tmhp.g_function.GFunction(N_1, N_2, B, H_b, D_b, r_b, alpha_s, k_s, t_max_s, dt_s, boundary_condition='UBWT', num=100)[source]¶

Bases: object

Dimensional g-function g(t) [m·K/W] of a bore field.

Built by precompute_gfunction(); callable like the scipy.interpolate.interp1d it replaces (g(t) for a scalar or an array of times [s]).

Values. pygfunction is evaluated on a log-spaced grid from t_min = 3600 s to the horizon, with g(0) = 0 prepended; between nodes the value is interpolated linearly in t (relative error ≤ 7e-4 against direct pygfunction evaluation for the default grid density). Below t_min the value is the straight line from (0, 0) to the first node: the model has no grout or fluid heat capacity, so the response in the first hour is only approximate.

Horizon. The grid ends at 1.5 * t_max_s. A request beyond it does not extrapolate: the grid is recomputed by pygfunction to cover the request (at least twice the previous horizon, keeping the node density per decade). For a 10×10 field this costs about 0.2 s. A simulation longer than t_max_s therefore gets the same values it would have got with a larger t_max_s.

Step mean. step_mean() returns (1/dt) ∫_τ^{τ+dt} g(s) ds, integrated exactly for the piecewise-linear g. This is the response a time-stepping model needs when its step n represents the interval [t_n, t_n + dt] and a load pulse starts at t_i: the mean wall temperature over the step is T_s - Σ Δq_i · step_mean(t_n - t_i, dt). It includes the step’s own pulse (τ = 0) and gives the same interval means for any dt that divides the load’s own resolution.

Any bore field. The constructor builds an N_1 × N_2 rectangle. from_field() takes any BoreField (arbitrary coordinates and per-borehole depths) and, with the 'MIFT' boundary condition, a BoreNetwork (pipes, flow, series or parallel connections). Grid, first hour, horizon and step_mean() behave the same way for every field. Such a g-function also returns the per-borehole split of the field load (borehole_load_shares()).

Ground coupling¶

Ground-coupling abstraction for borehole-heat-exchanger temporal superposition.

This module separates the ground thermal response (how the borehole-wall temperature responds to a history of ground loads) from the heat-pump cycle physics, so the response backend becomes swappable behind one small contract:

  • AggregateGFunctionCoupler (default) wraps a single field-average g-function g(tau) [m·K/W] and superposes load pulses with its step-mean response (the same convention as the inline superposition of GroundSourceHeatPump and GroundSourceHeatPumpEmpirical).

  • External packages (e.g. geolink) can implement the GroundCoupler protocol with a resolved multi-borehole network response — replacing the single lumped g-function with full borehole-to-borehole superposition — without this package depending on them (dependency inversion: tmhp defines the abstraction it needs; the richer implementation lives elsewhere and is injected).

Contract¶

A coupler owns the ground-load pulse history. The host (GroundSourceHeatPumpBoiler) calls GroundCoupler.reset() once per simulation and GroundCoupler.wall_temperature_rise() once per timestep with the current per-length ground load q_unit [W/m]; the coupler returns the borehole-wall temperature rise magnitude dT [K].

Time convention: step n stands for the interval [t_n, t_n + dt] and the returned dT is the wall-temperature rise averaged over that interval, including the response to the step’s own load q_unit. An external backend should return the same quantity so that results do not depend on which backend is injected.

Sign convention (matches the legacy GSHPB)¶

q_unit > 0 denotes heat extraction from the ground; the returned dT is the positive magnitude such that the disturbed wall temperature is T_wall = T_undisturbed - dT. (Note this is the opposite sign labelling from geolink’s q' > 0 = injection convention; a geolink coupler maps the sign at its boundary so the value returned here keeps the extraction-positive meaning.)

Borehole heat capacity (fluid-referenced wall temperature)¶

The host turns the wall temperature into the mean fluid temperature with the steady relation T_f = T_wall - q' R_b. A backend that models the heat capacity of the borehole (DynamicGFunction) therefore returns the fluid-referenced rise: the wall-temperature rise that gives the dynamic fluid temperature through that unchanged relation. It equals the physical wall rise once the borehole has settled (hours) and differs from it just after a load change, when the fluid has not yet followed. Such a backend reports the physical wall rise separately through the optional borehole_wall_rise(n, time_arr) (physical_wall_rise()), and says so with a true fluid_referenced attribute.

Runs that cover part of a step¶

The history holds one load per step. A host whose compressor runs for the first part of a step records the step-average load, but the fluid while it runs carries the running load. With a fluid-referenced response the host must therefore ask for the response over the run itself, under the running load (preview_wall_temperature_rise(..., window_s=...), see run_window_preview()): the first-instant terms of the response then cancel the host’s q' R_b for the same load. A backend that does not take window_s is asked for the whole step with the step-average load, as before.

class tmhp.ground_coupling.AggregateGFunctionCoupler(g_interp, pulse_tol=1e-06, dt_s=None)[source]¶

Bases: object

Single field-average g-function temporal superposition (default backend).

A pulse is recorded whenever the per-length load changes by more than pulse_tol; the pulse train is convolved with the step-mean response ḡ(τ; dt) = (1/dt) ∫_τ^{τ+dt} g (g_step_mean()). The value returned for step n is therefore the mean wall-temperature rise over the interval [t_n, t_n + dt], including the response to the step’s own load. (Before #96 G4 the pulse train was evaluated at the step start, g(t_n - t_i), so the current step’s pulse contributed g(0) ≈ 0 and the wall was reported up to ~0.8 K too warm at 25 W/m with hourly steps.)

On a uniform time grid the step-mean responses for every lag are computed once in reset() and each step is a contiguous dot product; a different time array passed later falls back to direct evaluation with the same step length. A non-uniform grid is rejected at reset().

Long runs. The dot product grows with the step index (a run of N steps costs N²/2: about 7 s for 525 600 steps, half an hour for 5 256 000). From FAST_MIN_STEPS steps on (a year at ten-minute steps), the same pulse sum is accumulated ahead of time by an online FFT convolution (tmhp._online_convolution), at a cost per step that does not depend on the run length (about 1 s and 10 s for the two runs above). It is not a load-aggregation scheme: nothing is averaged or truncated, and the values differ from the dot product by round-off only (1e-11 K over 250 000 steps). It applies while steps are recorded in increasing order on the grid given to reset(); any other use (a step recorded again, another time array) is answered by the dot product, with the same values.

History sum of a growing sequence against a fixed kernel, at a cost per step independent of its length.

Used by AggregateGFunctionCoupler for long runs. For a sequence p that is appended to step by step and a kernel k known in advance, OnlineHistory.history() returns

\[H_n = \sum_{i<n} p_i\, k_{n-i}\]

exactly (to round-off), without summing the whole history at every step.

After step t − 1 is known, the block of the last s entries, where s is the largest power of two dividing t, is convolved with k once and added to the next s steps. Every pair (entry, later step) is covered by exactly one block (the one at the highest binary digit in which the two indices differ), so H_n is complete once the entries before n have been folded in, and no later block changes it. Blocks are convolved with the FFT, which makes a run of N steps O(N log² N) instead of N²/2. This is the standard online (relaxed) convolution of computer algebra with one fixed operand.

A single step can take long when a large block is due (once, half-way through the run, a block of N/2 entries); only the total matters for a simulation.

Notes

With η = H / (ṁ c_p √(R_b R_a)) (Hellström 1991):

  • uniform borehole-wall temperature: R_b* = R_b η coth η (identical to pygfunction’s SingleUTube.effective_borehole_thermal_resistance);

  • uniform heat flux: R_b* = R_b + H² / (3 R_a (ṁ c_p)²) = R_b (1 + η²/3).

Since η coth η = 1 + η²/3 - η⁴/45 + …, the uniform-heat-flux value is never below the uniform-temperature one; they agree to first order and separate at low flow (large η).

Calculate the effective borehole thermal resistance R_b* [mK/W] for a Single U-tube.

Implements the full three-stage calculation in a single call:

Stage 1 — Fluid-to-pipe resistance (R_fp):

R_fp = R_conv + R_cond R_conv: convective resistance inside the pipe (Gnielinski correlation). R_cond: conductive resistance through the pipe wall (ln(r_out/r_in)/(2πk_p)).

Stage 2 — 2D cross-section (Local R_b via multipole method):

SingleUTube solves the steady-state 2D Laplace equation in the grout cross-section using Hellström’s multipole expansion at pygfunction’s default order J=2 (for the default GSHPB geometry at 24 L/min and H_b = 200 m, R_b* is 5e-5 relative below its J=10 value: 0.164398 vs 0.164405 m·K/W); see Javed and Spitler (2016) for an accuracy benchmark of this method. Boundary conditions: R_fp at each pipe surface, T=const at borehole wall. Outputs: Local R_b (fluid → borehole wall, cross-section only).

Stage 3 — Axial short-circuit correction (Effective R_b*):

pipe.effective_borehole_thermal_resistance() applies the Cimmino / Hellström analytical solution for axial fluid temperature variation and thermal short-circuiting between the two U-tube legs.

For a Single U-tube (series flow), each pipe leg carries the full borehole flow rate; m_flow_borehole is passed directly without division by 2.

Bore fields¶

See Ground response for usage and checks.

Bore-field layouts, pipe networks and the assembled field ground response.

BoreField describes where the boreholes are and how deep they go (any coordinates, per-borehole depth). BoreNetwork adds the pipes, the total flow and how the boreholes are connected (parallel headers or series chains). FieldGroundResponse puts them together into what a host model needs: the field g-function, the field resistance R_b for T_f = T_wall − q'·R_b (q' = Q/ΣH), the flow in each borehole and the split of the field load between boreholes.

All physics comes from pygfunction (Cimmino’s g-functions, multipole resistances, network fluid coupling); this module only arranges inputs and checks them.

Borehole heat capacity and short-time response¶

See Ground response for the model and its checks.

Borehole heat capacity and short-time ground response.

The steady borehole model used so far has two short-time defects that act in opposite directions:

  • the borehole-wall response is a line source evaluated at r_b, valid only after Fo = alpha t / r_b^2 of about 5 (several hours), and before one hour GFunction is a straight line from the origin; and

  • the fluid-to-wall temperature difference q' R_b appears instantly, whereas the fluid, pipe and grout store heat and the fluid temperature moves over one to three hours.

This module replaces both by one load-step response of the borehole cross section, the 1-D radial equivalent-cylinder model (ECM) of Xu and Spitler (2006):

fluid node (C_f) - R_in - node at r_eq (C_p)
  - grout annulus r_eq..r_b (k_g,eq, rho c_g) - wall node (r_b, no capacity)
  - ground r_b..r_far (k_s, rho c_s) - T = 0 at r_far

r_eq = sqrt(n) r_po keeps the grout cross-section (and so its heat capacity) equal to the real one, R_in = R_fp / n is the fluid-to-pipe resistance of the n pipes in parallel, and the equivalent grout conductivity is chosen so that the steady resistance of the model is exactly the target R_b (by default the multipole/Hellstrom value the host already uses):

R_b = R_in + ln(r_b / r_eq) / (2 pi k_g,eq)

The model is discretised with log-spaced finite volumes (conductance between cell centres 2 pi k / ln(r_{i+1}/r_i), exact for steady radial conduction) and backward Euler in time; energy is conserved to round-off.

Splicing onto the long-time g-function¶

For a unit load step the ECM gives the mean-fluid response S_cap(t) and the wall response W_cap(t) [m.K/W]. A g-function built from finite line sources behaves at short times like an infinite line source evaluated at r_b, which already fills the borehole with soil. The dynamic response is therefore the Yavuzturk-Spitler splice

fluid: S_dyn(t) = g(t) - G_ILS(r_b, t) + S_cap(t) wall : W_dyn(t) = g(t) - G_ILS(r_b, t) + W_cap(t)

with G_ILS = E1(r_b^2 / (4 alpha t)) / (4 pi k_s). Both corrections vanish at long times (S_cap -> R_b + G_ILS), so nothing changes after a few days. DynamicGFunction tabulates

K_f(t) = S_dyn(t) - R_b (“fluid-referenced” kernel, K_f(0) = -R_b) K_w(t) = W_dyn(t) (physical wall kernel)

and is a drop-in replacement for the g-function: a host that computes T_f = T_s - sum(dq * K_f) - q' R_b (the unchanged steady formula) obtains the dynamic mean fluid temperature, exact for piecewise-constant loads.

Limits¶

One-dimensional cross-section (the pipe layout enters only through R_b): against a 2-D finite-element reference the correction is, for most DN32 sections, within 5 % of R_b between 0.5 h and 2 h and about 2 % after 4 h (geolink technical note, see below; that comparison mapped the section to the local resistance, not to the effective R_b* used here). In the section model the fluid is one node; its transit through the U-tube is added separately (loop_outlet_response()) for one constant flow and a loop that keeps circulating. The fluid outside the borehole is not modelled.

Wraps a long-time field g-function g(t) [m.K/W] and the capacity response of one borehole section. It is callable and has step_mean like GFunction, so it can replace it in AggregateGFunctionCoupler and in the inline superposition of the other ground-source models.

Source-loop fluid and freezing limit¶

See Ground-loop liquid and its freezing limit for the rule and its basis.

Source-loop fluid of the liquid-source models: water or an antifreeze mixture.

The ground- and water-source models (GSHP, GSHPB, WSHPB) circulate a liquid between the source (a borefield, a coil lying in surface water) and the heat pump’s source-side heat exchanger. This module says what that liquid is, where it freezes and how cold it may run:

  • SourceFluid – freezing point, operating lower limit and the properties the source-side balances use;

  • resolve_source_fluid() – the constructor argument source_fluid ("water", "MEG-25%", "MPG-30%", "ethanol-20%", … or a SourceFluid; left out: 25 % ethylene glycol for the ground-source models, GROUND_LOOP_FLUID_DEFAULT, and water for the water-source boiler);

  • limit_duty_to_source_freeze() and source_freeze_limited() – the rule applied to every solved point.

Why a limit¶

Water freezes at 0 °C. Without the limit a model fed with a cold source returned a running cycle whose loop “water” left the evaporator at -3 °C or -12 °C. A real water loop does not do that: ice forms on the evaporator plates, which are colder than the liquid, the flow stops and the plate heat exchanger bursts. Loops that run near or below 0 °C are filled with an antifreeze mixture (propylene or ethylene glycol, ethanol), and the heat pump’s controller stops the compressor before the mixture can freeze.

Operating lower limit¶

T_min = T_freeze + 3 K (FREEZE_MARGIN_K), applied to the coldest liquid in the loop: in heating the liquid leaving the heat pump (T_bhe_f_in [°C]). Two things set the margin.

  • The evaporator surface is colder than the liquid leaving it. With the default source heat exchanger (UA = 0.18 * hp_capacity) and the default flow (3 K loop temperature difference at the rated source heat) the refrigerant evaporates 2.9 K below the leaving liquid at the rated duty (effectiveness 0.51: 3 K / 0.51 - 3 K). A 3 K margin therefore keeps the refrigerant, and with it the plate surface, at or above the freezing point at that duty.

  • Manufacturers of water-to-water and water-to-air units ask for antifreeze whenever the loop may enter the unit below 40-45 °F (4.4-7.2 °C), and for a freezing point 10-15 °F (5.6-8.3 K) below the lowest entering temperature (ClimateMaster and Carrier installation manuals: 15 °F; Enertech technical bulletin TB19.001: 10-15 °F; Trane GETB/WPHF: antifreeze below 45 °F). With the 3 K loop difference the leaving liquid is 3 K colder than the entering one, so those rules put the freezing point 2.6-5.3 K below the leaving liquid. 3 K is inside that range; for water it means: full duty down to about 6 °C entering, none below 3 °C.

A SourceFluid built by the caller can carry any other limit.

What happens at the limit¶

A variable-speed unit does not trip the moment the limit is reached: its controller lowers the compressor speed, the evaporator takes less heat from the liquid and the liquid leaves warmer. The models do the same (limit_duty_to_source_freeze()): the result is the largest duty whose loop liquid stays at or above T_min, with capacity_clamped == "max" and capacity_binding == "source_fluid_freezing". When no duty helps – the liquid already reaches the heat pump below the limit, or it leaves below the limit even at the compressor’s lowest speed – the compressor does not run: the off state with failure_reason == "source_fluid_freezing".

Properties¶

Water keeps the constants of tmhp.constants (1000 kg/m³, 4186 J/(kg K), 1.0 mPa s, 0.606 W/(m K)), so every result with the default fluid is unchanged by the property path. Mixtures are read from CoolProp’s incompressible mixtures (INCOMP::MPG[0.25] and the like; concentrations by mass) at PROPERTY_REFERENCE_C = 0 °C, the liquid inlet temperature of the ISO 13256-1 ground-loop and EN 14511 brine rating points and the cold end an antifreeze loop is designed for. They are constants in a run, like water’s.
