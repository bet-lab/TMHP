Scope and limits¶

TMHP aims at a useful accuracy from a nameplate (Minimal input), not at reproducing one machine from its drawings. That bargain has a price: some things are outside the models and are refused, and others are computed with a simplification. This page lists both, and for each one says how it shows in a result — the key to look at, the warning raised — so that a number is not trusted further than the model behind it.

The limits of the compressor envelope and the meaning of every failure_reason have their own pages (Reference points and operating limits, failure_reason semantics); they are linked here, not repeated.

What a running result claims¶

A row with hp_is_on == True, converged == True and failure_reason == "none" is a closed single-stage vapour-compression cycle: the energy balance holds, heat flows from warm to cold in every heat exchanger, and the compressor is inside its envelope. It does not claim that the request was met (capacity_clamped, Q_unmet [W]), that the compressor ran the whole time (runtime_fraction [-]), or that the efficiency fit was read inside its data (efficiency_extrapolated). Result keys describes these keys.

Refused: outside the models¶

Nothing in this table is returned as if it had been met. The request fails at construction with an error, returns the off state with a reason, or — where a smaller duty stays inside the limit — runs at that duty with the limit named in capacity_binding.

Transcritical cycles (R744 at heating temperatures)

Construction fails. A water heater: ValueError, the critical temperature is not above the delivery set point. The other families: ReferenceStateError (reference_capacity_inconsistent), naming the condensing temperature and the critical temperature

Refrigerants and CoolProp

User-defined HEOS:: mixtures

Not supported. The constructor raises ValueError (the default T_cond_max is not defined for a mixture); with T_cond_max=None it does not return

Refrigerants and CoolProp

Two-stage compression, vapour or liquid injection, economisers

The cycle is single-stage. Where a real unit would need them the point is outside the envelope: failure_reason is "pr_above_max" or "discharge_temp_above_max", or the duty is reduced (capacity_binding)

Reference points and operating limits

Operation beyond the compressor envelope

Condensing above T_cond_max, discharge above T_dis_max, a pressure ratio above 11, or the shipped isentropic fit below 0.30: reduced duty or the off state, never a running row

failure_reason semantics

A source loop colder than its liquid allows

The loop liquid is never colder than its freezing point plus 3 K (25 % ethylene glycol in GSHP and GSHPB: −8.0 °C; water in WSHPB: 3 °C). A heating duty that would chill it further is reduced (capacity_binding == "source_fluid_freezing"); when none fits the unit is off with failure_reason == "source_fluid_freezing"

Ground-loop liquid and its freezing limit

A machine that cannot exist

A nameplate the default compressor cannot deliver at the rating point, or one whose rating point is outside the envelope, is refused at construction (ReferenceStateError)

Compressor reference state

Inputs outside their physical range

ValueError before anything is computed (NaN, a negative capacity or flow, a kelvin value where °C is expected, a schedule of the wrong length)

failure_reason semantics

Water-source space conditioning

Not a released model: there is no class for it

The refrigerant cycle¶

Simplification

What it means

How it shows

Compressor efficiencies from one data set

Volumetric, isentropic and electro-mechanical efficiency are three fits to scroll-compressor calculation data over pressure ratio 1.50–7.15 and 33.8–72.5 rev/s. Outside that box the fit is held or continued, not measured

efficiency_extrapolated == True. A default machine with a refrigerant of low volumetric capacity (R134a, R1234yf) is rated above the box; give it a larger V_cmp_ref (Refrigerants and CoolProp, Where the defaults come from)

Adiabatic compressor

No shell heat loss: every loss ends up in the discharge gas

T_ref_cmp_out [°C] is an upper estimate, so the discharge limit binds early rather than late

Wet suction without an efficiency penalty

When the discharge control lets the suction go wet, the dry-suction efficiency fits are used unchanged

Rows with discharge_control == True are optimistic by a few percent in COP, and their duty at the edge of the limit is an upper estimate (Reference points and operating limits)

No pressure drop

Heat exchangers and lines are isobaric and the expansion is isenthalpic; superheat and subcooling are set values, not the result of a valve or charge model

Not flagged. P_ref_cmp_out [Pa] equals the condensing pressure in every row

One conductance per heat exchanger

Each coil is a single ε-NTU exchanger against the refrigerant’s saturation temperature, with a UA that follows the flow. Desuperheating and subcooling zones, fin geometry and refrigerant distribution are not resolved

Not flagged. The default UA rules and their spread are in Where the defaults come from

Quasi-steady cycle in time

A dynamic step solves a steady cycle; refrigerant migration and the warm-up after a start are not simulated. Thermal mass is in the tank water and the borehole only

Starts cost nothing in a dynamic boiler run (hp_starts [-] counts them). Below the minimum speed, ASHP and GSHP rows and every analyze_steady apply the EN 14825 degradation instead (E_cycling_loss [W])

No standby power

An off row draws nothing: crankcase heater, controls and an idling pump are not modelled, except through the EN 14825 Cc of a boiler’s steady on/off mean

E_tot [W] is 0 on off rows; add a standby load yourself for an annual balance

Air side¶

Simplification

What it means

How it shows

Dry air at one standard atmosphere

Every air coil (ASHP both coils, ASHPB, the GSHP indoor coil, also in the empirical GSHP) takes the density of the air it draws in — outdoor air at T0, room air at T_a_room — as dry ideal-gas air at 101 325 Pa (tmhp.air_properties.air_density()). The specific heat is constant (1005 J/(kg K)); humidity and altitude are left out, and fan power does not follow the density

Not flagged. dV_ou_a [m3/s] and dV_iu_a [m3/s] are volumes at the coil inlet; at 1 600 m the real density is 18 % lower (Air density)

Sensible cooling only

The indoor coil is dry: no dehumidification, so the cooling duty is the sensible one

Q_ref_iu [W] in cooling is below a catalogue’s total capacity at a low sensible heat ratio (Validation, Cooling and latent load)

Frost as a correlation (ASHP)

Below 5 °C outdoor the EnergyPlus frost multipliers are applied to a frost-free cycle. They depend on outdoor temperature and humidity, not on the coil the model solves, and a defrost is in the mean, not resolved in time

defrost_fraction [-], frost_capacity_factor [-], E_defrost [W]; Q_ref_iu_frost_free [W] is the cycle that was solved (Air-source heat pump (ASHP — space conditioning))

No frost correction for the air-source boiler

ASHPB solves the frost-free cycle at every outdoor temperature

Not flagged. Below about 5 °C outdoor its COP and capacity are those of a coil that never frosts

Ground and source water¶

Simplification

What it means

How it shows

Loop liquid with constant properties

The properties of source_fluid are those of the mixture at 0 °C (water: 1000 kg/m³, 4186 J/(kg K)) and do not change with temperature during a run. UA_ground is not derated for an antifreeze’s poorer film coefficient

Not flagged. T_bhe_f_in [°C] is the loop temperature the freezing limit acts on

Parts of the ground model that still assume water

The hydraulic pump model, the flow-dependent borehole resistance, the coupled ground-flow solver and the dynamic borehole response use the properties of water also for the default glycol

Pump power of the hydraulic model is understated for a glycol loop, and loop temperatures differ by about 0.1 K; a model whose source_fluid you chose says so in a UserWarning (Ground-loop liquid and its freezing limit)

Ground as a line-source response

Homogeneous ground at a constant undisturbed temperature, boreholes through a g-function and a borehole resistance; no seasonal surface wave, no gradient, no groundwater flow

T_bhe [°C]; the limits of the dynamic borehole are listed in Ground response, Limits

analyze_steady has no ground history

A steady ground-source point is solved against the temperature you give (T_source, T_bhe_wall, or the undisturbed Ts); the ground does not respond

T_bhe [°C] of a steady row without T_bhe_wall is back-calculated from the load and can lie above Ts while heat is extracted. Use analyze_dynamic for the ground response

Source water as a temperature (WSHPB)

The source is a prescribed water temperature around a submerged coil; the water body itself is not modelled

Ts [°C] repeats the input (Water-source heat pump boiler (WSHPB))

Demand side¶

Simplification

What it means

How it shows

No building model

ASHP and GSHP take the load and the room temperature as inputs. A load that is not met does not cool the room

Q_unmet [W] reports the shortfall; feeding it back is the caller’s job (Integrations)

Fully mixed tank by default

The boilers’ tank is one node; only GSHPB offers a multi-node tank (tank_model). Tank properties are those of water at constant density and specific heat

T_tank_w [°C] is the single tank temperature (Ground-source heat pump boiler (GSHPB), Tank backends)

Hot water that is not delivered

A draw the tank cannot serve at the set point is not an error

Q_dhw_unmet [W] per step and one DHWUnmetWarning per run (Was the hot water delivered?)

Time step and thermostat

The thermostat switches inside a step, but one cycle is solved per step

runtime_fraction [-]; step-size guidance in Time step and the thermostat

What has been compared with data¶

The air-source families are compared with manufacturer catalogues, with no parameter fitted to them; the ground- and water-source families share the same cycle core but have not been compared with unit data. The numbers, the units held back and the reasons are in Validation.

A short checklist for any result¶

ok = (df["failure_reason"] == "none") & df["converged"]   # an operating point (or a legitimate off row)
averaged = df["runtime_fraction [-]"] < 1                 # a time average, not a continuous point

Then, where they exist: capacity_clamped == "max" for a row that delivered less than asked, Q_unmet [W] or Q_dhw_unmet [W] for demand that was not served, efficiency_extrapolated and discharge_control for points at the edge of the compressor data. Warnings raised during a run (UserWarning at construction, RuntimeWarning for a point that fell back to off) say the same things once, in words.
