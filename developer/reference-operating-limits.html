Reference points and operating limits¶

Developer naming rules¶

rated means a manufacturer/design reference operating point, not a maximum operating limit. ref denotes a fixed normalization point in flow scaling APIs. Physical/control limits use explicit _min/_max names; fixed operating commands use _constant or _setpoint. Ratios state their denominator, for example ground_flow_ratio or fan_flow_ratio_to_ref. Never change a normalization denominator when expanding an operating range.

Existing thermodynamic identifiers containing ref (refrigerant), such as T_ref_sat_K and actual m_dot_ref, are thermodynamic states, not scaling references. m_dot_ref_rated is the fixed refrigerant mass-flow reference for ground-HX scaling; when omitted it is solved at the compressor reference state, so no external reference run is needed. V_cmp_ref is swept displacement per revolution; it does not define speed limits. Compressor limits remain rps_min/rps_max.

Ground-loop API and migration¶

GSHP and GSHPB accept independent field-total values in L/min:

  • ground_flow_ref_lpm: fixed water normalization reference.

  • ground_flow_constant_lpm: actual constant-flow setpoint (defaults to ref).

  • ground_flow_min_lpm and ground_flow_max_lpm: variable-flow limits.

For the current paper, these are 24, 24, 9.6 and 36 L/min. A 36 L/min candidate has ratio 1.5 to the unchanged 24 L/min reference. The constant strategy always uses 24 L/min. Constant setpoints must lie within the declared control interval; the reference remains a normalization and interpolation anchor. Do not infer an additional hardware cap from the reference. Prescribed variable candidates must lie inside the explicit interval; only floating-point endpoint rounding is tolerated.

The previous dV_b_f_lpm and ratio-limit constructor inputs remain adapters, with DeprecationWarning on explicit use. New absolute inputs take precedence. Omitting all new inputs gives the default reference flow (GSHP: capacity-based, 0.75 × hp_capacity / (ρc × 3 K), 14.3 L/min for 4 kW — 20.04 L/min until November 2026, see Ground-source heat pump (GSHP — space conditioning); GSHPB 24 L/min; ratio interval 0.2–1.2). dV_b_f_m3s remains a reference-flow attribute for compatibility, never the final candidate’s mutable state.

analyze_steady(..., ground_flow_lpm=24) forces an actual candidate; the previous ground_flow_ratio argument remains a deprecated ratio-to-reference adapter. Results expose ground_flow [m3/s], ground_flow_ratio, ground_flow_at_min and ground_flow_at_max. Historical result keys dV_bhe_f [m3/s] and ground_flow_ratio remain aliases. A failed candidate reports zero delivered flow and NaN ratio/COP, while retaining candidate diagnostics.

Internally, the optimizer varies actual volume flow [m3/s]. Pump pressure drop uses actual flow. Rb* interpolation uses branch mass flow [kg/s] and pins the reference/setpoint resistances; no normalization denominator enters the Rb* physics. Grid bounds cover the control interval and compatibility anchors. Ground UA uses fixed water/refrigerant reference flows and permits UA above its reference value. No ratio-to-reference clamp at one is applied.

Indoor approach search domain¶

GSHP exposes indoor_approach_min_K / indoor_approach_max_K separately from airflow limits. Defaults are 1–25 K (1–20 K until November 2026: the rated duty of the default indoor coil at 400 cfm/ton needs 21.6 K). An expanded water-flow interval can require an indoor approach above 20 K to close the HX while respecting the compressor pressure-ratio floor. The current 26 °C cooling study explicitly uses 1–25 K (minimum evaporating temperature 1 °C). These are numerical cycle search limits, not fan or reference limits; they must be declared alongside the control envelope. A custom-curve regression demonstrates a feasible PLR 0.3 / 36 L/min point above 20 K. With the new generic fan minimum of 15%, the current paper has no steady solution at PLR 0.3; its unchanged pressure-ratio floor and both HX duties cannot be satisfied together. This is recorded as an infeasible requested point, with no cycling model or constraint relaxation.

Fan API and migration¶

ASHP accepts dV_ou_fan_a_ref/min/max and dV_iu_fan_a_ref/min/max; GSHP accepts the indoor trio; ASHPB accepts dV_fan_a_ref/min/max [m3/s]. Existing *_rated inputs remain supported reference aliases; an explicit *_ref takes precedence. Cross-sectional area and reference fan power are computed at that fixed reference, independently of the solver limits.

The common HX solver accepts dV_fan_ref/min/max plus custom_fan_curve. The generic ASHRAE Appendix G Method 2 correlation is used only over 0.15–1.0 of reference airflow. Requested limits below/above that range are intersected with it; incompatible limits raise ValueError. calc_fan_operating_point exposes actual flow, power and fan_flow_min_limit / fan_flow_max_limit. A nondefault user polynomial or explicit curve_type="custom" marks a custom model whose validity and limits are the caller’s responsibility; that model may allow max > ref. Reference and hardware maximum remain independent inputs.

Constant-speed fans¶

A single-speed (or single-stage) fan is min == max: pass dV_*_fan_a_min=dV_*_fan_a_max=<flow> and set the reference dV_*_fan_a_rated (or _ref) to the same flow, so the coil UA and the fan power are normalised at the flow the fan actually runs at. Equal limits are accepted by resolve_fan_flow_limits for every fan; min > max and a zero maximum remain input errors, and the generic ASHRAE curve still requires the flow to lie inside 0.15–1.0 of the reference. The coil cannot change its airflow, so a duty is carried only through the approach temperature: ASHPB, GSHP and ASHP solve that single approach (the band of approaches the fan can serve collapses to a point) and report the duty exactly. A request the coil cannot carry is answered as for a variable fan: ASHPB runs at the largest duty the coil carries (capacity_clamped == "max", capacity_binding == "outdoor_coil"), GSHP and ASHP fail with failure_reason == "hx_not_converged".

A constant-speed fan sits at both limits, so both ou_fan_flow_min_limit and ou_fan_flow_max_limit (iu_* for an indoor fan) are true; the direction in which a coil duty misses is in capacity_margin_W of the coil solve. Fan power is the power curve evaluated at the flow ratio, so at the reference flow it is curve(1) times the reference power: 0.998262 for the default single-zone VAV surrogate and 0.9991 for the ASHRAE correlation. Pass vsd_coeffs={"c1": 1, "c2": 0, "c3": 0, "c4": 0, "c5": 0, "curve_type": "custom"} for a power that is exactly the rated value at every flow.

AirSourceHeatPump has two coils and searches two approach temperatures. With one constant-speed fan the feasible approaches lie on a curve, with two on a single point, and neither a grid nor a simplex lands on a set of zero width. The model therefore nests two band solves: the coil with the narrower fan range is closed first (its approach is a root of that coil’s capacity margin), and the other coil’s band is solved at that solution and searched for the least electrical input per unit of heat. Both fans fixed costs about 30 cycle evaluations (0.3 s); one fixed fan about 80 (under 1 s). A fan that can modulate over less than 2:1 (NARROW_FAN_FLOW_RATIO) uses the same search to seed the ordinary one whenever the coarse pre-scan finds no operating point.

Note

With a constant-speed fan the approach-temperature search domain (dT_approach_bounds, 1–30 K by default) is the only freedom the coil has, so it can become the capacity limit: the largest duty is then what the coil carries at the upper bound, reported with capacity_clamped == "max" and capacity_binding == "cycle". The ceiling is one least-lift solve (tmhp.AirSourceHeatPump.max_capacity()); widen the bounds if the coil is meant to run a larger approach.

The unchanged empirical equation is

\[P^* = 0.0013 + 0.1470x + 0.9506x^2 - 0.0998x^3,\quad x=\dot V_{actual}/\dot V_{ref}.\]

Source: ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1-2016 Appendix G, Table G3.1.3.15 Method 2. Method 1’s rounded table gives approximately 0.03, 0.30 and 1.00 power at 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 airflow. Raw table parity is separate from operating bounds. ASHRAE 2025 Fundamentals Chapter 19 describes measured/regressed fan curves and cautions against extrapolation below a minimum ratio, with 0.15 as an example. This motivates the generic control assumption; it is not universal heat-pump hardware data. 90.1-2022 Addendum u adds supporting 15% turndown evidence for multizone VAV. The informative foreword discusses 16% power at 15% airflow; this is not a normative 16% requirement. Amended Section 6.5.3.2.1(b) deletes the old 30% power sentence without inserting a 16% sentence. Its minimum-airflow provision includes the design minimum outdoor-air rate and is an upper limit on the selectable minimum, not a universal command to clamp every fan to at least 15%. Single-zone VAV is outside the foreword’s described change. No universal heat-pump electrical floor is inferred or added. Original Windows Chrome PDF/HTML highlights and capture metadata are archived in references/fan_model/. The generic 0.15–1.0 envelope remains a TMHP modeling choice. Direct equation values at x=0.10/0.15/0.50/1.00 are 0.0254062/0.044401675/0.299975/0.9991, without renormalizing coefficients.

calc_UA_from_dV_fan supports the new dV_fan_ref keyword and its existing rated positional argument. UA scaling stays independent of fan power bounds and permits ratios above one for custom curves. Fan-power dictionaries accept fan_ref_flow_rate / fan_ref_power plus historical rated/design aliases, and optional fan_min_flow_rate / fan_max_flow_rate. HX solvers report bounded actual airflow and signed duty residuals. A clamp with unmatched heat remains infeasible and cannot enter a finite optimization objective.

Audit and validation¶

The repository audit is in docs/audits/reference-rated-max.md. It inventories reference/rated/design/ratio/max identifiers in all five released heat-pump families, common HX/fan and ground helpers, and compressor speed handling. WSHPB has fixed (capacity-scaled by default) surface-water/tank UAs and explicit speed limits; it contains no fan solver or rated-flow ceiling to expand. Numerical regression tests retain old constructor behavior, and new tests verify identical component physics at an identical actual operating point across control strategies.

Compressor efficiency baseline and speed¶

All five heat-pump models resolve eta_cmp_isen, eta_cmp_vol and eta_cmp set to None to the default maps of tmhp.compressor_efficiency (coefficient set ossorio-r290-te-2026-11-21: one R290 scroll, the same maps for every refrigerant and compressor). These represent isentropic, volumetric and electro-mechanical efficiency, respectively; eta_cmp is fitted on the power of compressor and inverter together, so the compressor input is that at the inverter, with no separate drive model. The defaults are called model(PR, rps, state) with the suction state (tmhp.compressor_efficiency.SuctionState, which holds the evaporating saturation temperature T_evap_K). Each input also accepts a scalar, a function(PR) or a function(PR, rps). Every evaluated value must satisfy 0 < eta <= 1: a value above one is set to one, a nonpositive or nonfinite value makes the operating point infeasible, and exceptions raised inside a user function are preserved.

The defaults take the shaft speed rps [rev/s] as an absolute speed, relative to a fixed 70 rev/s; nothing is read relative to rps_rated. When omitted, the reference state solves the speed delivering hp_capacity and its m_dot_ref_rated at the family’s rating condition (Compressor reference state). Explicit rated speed and refrigerant flow remain available. The reference solve does not change rps_min; an omitted rps_max is derived from the rated speed it finds (see the operating envelope below).

The default maps carry the domain of their fitting data (validity_domain, a tmhp.compressor_efficiency.MapDomain: the speeds 30–110 rev/s and the convex hull of the measured points in pressure ratio and evaporating temperature). Outside it the maps are followed as they are – nothing is held, clamped or refused there – and efficiency_extrapolated(model, PR, rps, T_evap_C) reports whether a point lies outside it. The earlier default models (the semi-empirical models of 2026-11-15, the quadratic map of 2026-10-06 with its validity-domain guard, and the correlations before it) are no longer in the library; their archives remain under validation/coefficients/, and the code that evaluates the quadratic map and the correlations is kept as records in validation/coefficients/recorded_maps/.

GSHP now solves speed using m_dot = V_cmp_ref * rho_suction * eta_v * rps at each speed candidate, and evaluates isentropic efficiency before calculating heat duty. Refrigerant work includes isentropic losses; compressor electricity is refrigerant work divided by electro-mechanical efficiency exactly once. Electro-mechanical losses remain outside the refrigerant heat balance. The capacity clamp returned by the shared speed solver reports actual delivered duty. Coupled ground-flow operation rejects clamped points that cannot meet the requested duty. Its failed points retain unavailable COP/power semantics.

GSHP’s former fixed default displacement of 100 cm3/rev over-sized its 4 kW default capacity once speed bounds were enforced. An omitted displacement now uses the same capacity-based default as the other models; explicitly supplied displacement is unchanged. Deprecated GSHP eta_v / eta_em scalar inputs map to the new interface, with explicit new inputs taking precedence.

The audit of the efficiency baseline of 2026-10 (source module SHA, original checkout commit and coefficient snapshot) is recorded under docs/audits/compressor-efficiency-*. The efficiency models describe compressor modulation, not cycling losses or on-off duty averaging.

At the pressure-ratio floor, GSHP recomputes permitted subcooling from the projected condenser temperature and its physical sink approach. Rejected trial approaches therefore cannot change the refrigerant liquid state at identical projected conditions. Coupled optimization explicitly includes the ground-HX duty-equality boundary at the PR floor; the shipped efficiency coefficients and existing HX, load and optimization verification tolerances remain unchanged.

Compressor efficiency range (2026-11-12)¶

The temporary 2026-10-07 policy of passing fitted efficiencies above one through unchanged has ended (issue #96). Values above one are set to one, the bound each efficiency approaches; nonpositive and nonfinite efficiencies remain invalid.

Condensing limit (T_cond_max)¶

Every published compressor envelope ends at a maximum condensing temperature, which is a maximum high-side pressure: the shell, the scroll set and the motor are built for it and the unit’s high-pressure switch stops the compressor just above it. Before this limit the boilers returned normal operation at pressures no compressor is built for – R410A charging a tank to 65 °C at 70 °C condensing (4.76 MPa, 97 % of the critical pressure), R32 at 77.9 °C, a top-coil stratified tank with R134a at 96 °C.

No published envelope reaches it, and next to the critical point the condenser has almost no latent heat left. R744 gets 26.4 °C: its heat pumps are transcritical, which the subcritical cycle does not describe.

The check compares pressures: a running state is outside when its condensing pressure exceeds the dew pressure at T_cond_max (by more than the pressure of CONDENSING_TOL_K = 0.01 K). For a zeotropic blend the envelope temperature is the dew point while the cycle’s own condensing temperature is the bubble point, lower by the glide: R407C reaches the 65 °C limit when T_ref_cond_sat_l is 61.2 °C. It is judged before the discharge temperature, since it depends neither on the efficiency model nor on the discharge-temperature control (the suction state the control moves does not change the condensing pressure).

The condensing temperature is the demand-side temperature plus the condenser approach, and the approach shrinks with the duty, so the limit is answered like the other limits a smaller duty cures:

  • ASHP, GSHP — a candidate above the limit is rejected inside the operating-point search. When that leaves a capacity ceiling the family cannot name ("cycle"), the same request is solved once more without the limit; a ceiling that then rises is reported with capacity_binding == "condensing_temperature". With no deliverable duty the result is the off state with failure_reason == "condensing_temp_above_max".

  • ASHPB, GSHPB, WSHPB — the solved operating point is checked; analyze_steady and each dynamic step then run at the duty that condenses on the limit, with capacity_clamped == "max" and capacity_binding == "condensing_temperature". That duty is not searched with the ladder and bisection of the other limits: the refused request and the lowest duty of the ladder give two points of the condensing temperature over the duty, the secant gives the duty on the limit, and one solve confirms it (exactly, for a mixed tank whose approach is Q / UA; a stratified tank’s coil-side water warms with the duty, and the secant is repeated). Three cycle solves for a step that runs against the limit, two for one that cannot run. When the tank itself is at the limit no duty runs: the off state with failure_reason == "condensing_temp_above_max".

  • A machine whose rating point condenses above the limit is refused at construction (ReferenceStateError naming the standard, the condensing temperature and T_cond_max): the nameplate cannot be defined outside the envelope. An 8 kW R410A boiler with a 500 W/K tank coil (16 K approach, 71 °C at W55) is such a machine.

The thermostat band is capped at the envelope’s cut-out. A boiler whose upper set point lies above what its compressor reaches does not charge the tank to it; a real controller stops at the unit’s maximum. The thermostat has to know that temperature: a dynamic step solves one cycle, at the tank temperature its run averages to (tmhp.dynamic_context.resolve_step_operation()), and applies its heat over the step, so a cut-out the thermostat does not see is overrun inside a long step by an amount that depends on the step (8 kW R410A, 100 L, 65 °C set point, called at 54.9 °C: the tank stopped at 62.1 °C after 0.84 kWh with 60 s steps and at 65.0 °C after 1.19 kWh with 600 s steps, and with short steps every later step was a called-but-refused one). So at construction (tmhp.compressor_envelope.limit_tank_setpoints_to_envelope()):

  • the cut-out is the tank temperature at which the nameplate duty condenses on the limit, T_cond_max (as the cycle’s bubble point) less hp_capacity / K_tank_hx (K_tank_hx: the heat the tank heat exchanger carries per kelvin of condensing temperature above the tank water) – 59.9 °C for R410A, 60.0 °C for R32, 69.9 °C for R290 with the tank heat exchanger of before November 2026 (a 5 K approach, no circulating water);

  • a T_tank_w_upper_bound above it is replaced by it, and a T_tank_w_lower_bound that is not below it is moved down to keep the width of the band (the ASHPB’s 60–65 °C becomes 54.9–59.9 °C with R410A);

  • a UserWarning says what was changed, and T_tank_w_setpoints_requested keeps the values asked for.

Up to the cut-out the machine runs at its nameplate duty and the thermostat ends the run inside the step, as at any set point: the same machine, one hour from 50 °C, takes 1.165 kWh and stops at 59.9 °C with 60 s and with 600 s steps alike. A set point inside the envelope is untouched and nothing warns – 65 °C with R290 or R134a, which is why ASHPB, GSHPB and WSHPB default to R290. The duty reduction above then serves analyze_steady and the steps that go past the cut-out all the same: a stratified tank whose coil-side water is hotter than the sensor, a minimum run time, a set point moved after construction.

Charging past the cut-out at reduced duty (condensing_limit_control="reduce_duty" on ASHPB, GSHPB and WSHPB; the default "stop" is everything above). The cut-out is where the nameplate duty meets the limit. A fixed-speed unit stops there. An inverter unit lowers its frequency and keeps charging at the largest duty the limit leaves, which is what analyze_steady returns against a hotter tank; ending the charge at the nameplate cut-out understates the temperature such a unit reaches on the heat pump alone (R32, 15 kW: 58.0 against about 63 °C). With "reduce_duty" a dynamic run follows the unit along the limit.

The limit as a duty. The cycle condenses at the tank temperature plus Q / K_tank_hx, so on the limit the compressor delivers Q_lim(T) = K_tank_hx (T_reach − T), with T_reach the bubble point of the limit pressure (tmhp.compressor_envelope.condensing_tank_reach()). The nameplate cut-out is where Q_lim equals hp_capacity. Above it the tank follows C_tank dT/dt = K_tank_hx (T_reach − T) + P(T) (P: draw, loss, subsystems): a first-order approach to T_reach with the time constant C_tank / K_tank_hx, 295 s for the default 151 L tank of a 15 kW unit. An explicit step that applies one cycle’s heat over an hour cannot follow that; this is why the default caps the thermostat at the cut-out.

Inside the step. The in-step thermostat (tmhp.dynamic_context.simulate_thermostat_step(), duty_limit) already integrates a linear tank equation exactly between its events. The stretch on the limit is one more linear equation, so it is integrated exactly too, with the knee (the cut-out) as an event. Within that event model – the free heat P(T) a straight line in the tank temperature, the condensing limit the one that governs the stretch (checked per source state at three tank temperatures; Where it applies below has the check and the case it does not see) – the run time and the heat of a step are exact whatever the step: on the charges below, 60 s, 600 s and 3600 s steps give the same run time to the second and the same heat to 0.05 %. One stretch is deliberately not the events’: the hold of a step in which the tank first cooled to the stop takes its heat from the balance that closes the step, and so depends on the step length (Where the charge ends; 0.002 % of the heat of an hour in the case measured there).

That is a statement about the events, not about every tank. The tank temperature a step ends at does not come from them: as on every step of either option it is one implicit solve of the tank balance with the step’s mean heat and the loss at the end-of-step temperature. The two agree as far as the loss changes little over a step, which is the kernel’s property and the same with "stop". On the limit the duty itself follows the tank temperature, so a difference in the temperature a step ends at carries into the next step’s heat; a tank that loses a large part of the compressor’s heat (the last row: 3 of 15 kW) needs short steps with either option.

Where the charge ends. The compressor has a smallest duty it runs at continuously: its speed floor, or the envelope’s lower side where the discharge runs too hot at low speed (tmhp.compressor_envelope.smallest_continuous_duty(); the probe is the lowest rung of the duty reduction’s ladder, 10 % of the nameplate, so the floor is the one analyze_steady has). The charging limit is the tank temperature at which that duty condenses on the limit, T_reach − Q_min / K_tank_hx (tmhp.compressor_envelope.smallest_duty_on_condensing_limit(): usually three cycle solves). The search stays where a tank can be: for a refrigerant whose limit lies high enough (R600a with T_cond_max above about 101 °C) the smallest duty would meet the limit above 100 °C, and the charge then ends at the boiling point, as it does without a condensing limit. Above the charging limit nothing runs. Q_min depends on the source – denser suction gas from a warmer source gives more duty at the lowest speed – so a step uses the limit of its own source, and the thermostat’s cap is that limit at one stated reference: the rating point’s outdoor air for ASHPB, the source at Ts for GSHPB and WSHPB (charge_limit_reference_C; tmhp.compressor_envelope.limit_tank_setpoints_to_charge_limit()). Where a step’s own stop lies below the set point the compressor stops there while the call stands, and the row says hp_stop_reason == "condensing_limit" ("boiling_limit" when the stop that acted was the boiling point: the row names the stop the step was resolved with, not the nameplate cut-out). What follows depends on the minimum rest time. Without one (min_off_time_s = 0, the default) the compressor holds the tank at the stop, cycling at Q_min against the tank’s losses. That hold is the mean of on/off cycling with no differential and no rest, reported as one run: hp_starts [-] counts the start of the hold only, runtime_fraction [-] is the share of the time the compressor is on, and the carried state says “running” with a phase age that counts from that start – the timers describe the hold, not a compressor that has run all that time. With a minimum rest time the cycling is not something the compressor can do, and the step resolves it as it happens: stop at the stop, rest for min_off_time_s while the tank cools, restart (a counted start), run back along the limit. Run time, starts and timers then describe the same compressor (R32, 35 °C outdoor, both minimum times 1800 s, three 1200 s steps from 61.0 °C with a small draw: 27 s to the stop at 61.3 °C, 1800 s of rest, a second run of 201 s; the hold of the same case runs 521 s as one start). A compressor on the limit near the stop runs at its lowest speed, and the cycle there can have a little more than the duty it is asked for (6 parts in a million for that unit at the stop). The row then takes that cycle’s rates – heat, power, flows – at the duty asked, with the cycle’s COP and states, so that the tank gets what the events integrated and no more: a hold stays at the stop, and the run times are those of the events. The excess is not small by construction; what bounds it is the 0.1 % within which a cycle counts as delivering the duty asked (tmhp.dynamic_context.DUTY_MATCH_RTOL), so the rates are lowered by at most 0.0999 %, and a cycle further off withholds the step (Where it applies). Measured near that bound: the same unit with a speed floor of 8 rev/s (rps_min=8) at 45 °C outdoor, holding at its stop of 62.48 °C, is asked for 5,382.86 W and its cycle delivers 5,388.21 W, so the rates are taken times 0.99901 (0.0992 %). Heat, power and refrigerant flow are scaled together: heat over power is still the COP of the row, and the heat is the flow times the enthalpy difference of the row’s states. The multi-node tanks of GSHPB, which keep "stop", hold the water around their coil as a mean whatever the rest time, as before, and so does a step with reduced-duty charging withheld at its nameplate cut-out (it is the step of "stop"). If the tank loses more at the stop than Q_min (a draw), the compressor is not held to that duty: it runs on, the tank cools, the limit leaves it more, and the tank settles below the stop where the limit’s duty is the loss (R32, 35 °C outdoor, a 10.5 kW draw: the stop is 61.3 °C with 7.9 kW, the tank settles at 60.1 °C). Above the stop the compressor does not run at all, so on the limit it never delivers less than Q_min; a tank found above the stop of its step (the source warmed, or a collector heated it, or the run was started there) waits until it has cooled to it, and is held from then on. The heat of the hold in that step is the one the step’s closing balance needs to end at the stop: the loss at the stop for the whole step, less the heat the tank gave up cooling to it. It is taken from the boiler’s own implicit tank balance, not from the straight line the events use for the tank’s free heat, and the same number decides whether the compressor starts in that step at all, so that a tank whose free heat is not a straight line (a subsystem) is not left without a start in a step its own balance closes below the stop. Where that balance needs more than the compressor gives at the stop’s duty in what is left of the step, it runs through what is left instead of cycling; the step then closes below the stop with the compressor running, and the next step charges on from there. The closing balance is asked only at a start the events give: it sets that start’s heat and can take the start back, but a step in which the events’ tank does not reach the stop has no start, even if the boiler’s own balance would pass the stop in it. The compressor then starts at the beginning of the next step, as the event model has it for any switching point of a tank whose free heat is not a straight line, with either option. (The events have the tank lose more while it is warmer than the stop; the implicit balance that sets the end-of-step temperature takes the loss of the whole step at that temperature. With the heat of the events’ hold the 15 kW R32 unit at 35 °C outdoor, cooling from 61.8 °C to its stop of 61.3034 °C in a 600 s step, ended the step 1.3e-4 K above the stop, and the next step counted a stop and a second start; it now ends at the stop and the run has one start, with 60 s, 600 s and 3600 s steps.) That heat is the closing balance’s, not the events’, so it depends on the step length as that balance does: a long step takes the loss of the cooling at the stop’s temperature, a run of short ones at the warmer temperatures the tank passes through. The hour of this case delivers 3624.30, 3624.29, 3624.22 and 3624.22 kJ with 10, 60, 600 and 3600 s steps – 79 J or 0.002 % apart, 458.12 to 458.11 s of run time – and ends at the stop with all four. Where the tank reaches the stop in the last moments of a step, so that by the closing balance it is not there yet, the compressor starts in the next step. A step with reduced-duty charging withheld holds at its nameplate cut-out with the kernel of "stop", unchanged, which in the same situation still counts that second start.

Where it applies. The events take the condensing limit’s duty for the whole stretch from the nameplate cut-out (the knee) to the stop. That is the tank’s path only where the condensing limit is the limit that governs that stretch. With a cold source another one can hold the compressor below it – the source fluid’s freezing limit, the pressure-ratio ceiling, the outdoor coil at its largest airflow, a discharge-temperature ceiling, the compressor’s own speed ceiling – and a step integrated along the condensing limit would then credit the tank with heat the cycle does not deliver. So the stretch is qualified before it is used, once per source state (tmhp.compressor_envelope.condensing_stretch()): the step’s cycle is solved at the knee, at the middle of the stretch and at the stop, each time asked for the duty the condensing limit leaves there. The stretch qualifies if all three run, deliver that duty to 0.1 % (tmhp.dynamic_context.DUTY_MATCH_RTOL; the measured ratio is 1.000000 where the condensing limit governs) and name no limit but the condensing one. The source state is the one the smallest duty is found for, and the verdict is kept with it: the outdoor air for ASHPB, the source water for WSHPB, the ground as the step finds it for GSHPB (every step that gets to the limit). That verdict depends on the machine and its source state alone – not on the step, and not on where in the stretch the tank is. Whether a given step of a qualified source state is withheld also depends on the step itself, through the check of its own cycles described below.

Where the stretch does not qualify, reduced-duty charging is withheld for that step. The step is then solved exactly as "stop" solves it: the same call of the kernel with the nameplate cut-out, the same cycle solves, the same heat, electricity and labels. The thermostat band is not changed (it stays the one the model was built with), so a call for heat above the nameplate cut-out stands and is not met while the source state lasts. Two keys say what happened: reduced_duty_fraction [-] is 0, and reduced_duty_withheld names what withheld the charging on every row in which the nameplate cut-out then acted (the compressor stopped at it, or stayed off above it; hp_stop_reason == "condensing_limit"). The name is the capacity_binding of the first of the three cycles that was held below the limit’s duty ("source_fluid_freezing", "pressure_ratio", "outdoor_coil", "discharge_temperature"; "compressor" for the speed ceiling), or the failure_reason of the first that did not run ("source_fluid_freezing", "discharge_temp_above_max"); "no_smaller_duty" where no continuous duty below the nameplate runs at all. On every other row the key is None. That includes every row whose stop was the boiling point (hp_stop_reason == "boiling_limit"): a machine whose nameplate duty meets the condensing limit at or above 100 °C (the water-source unit with R600a and T_cond_max = 108 °C: nameplate cut-out 101.0 °C) never gets to that limit, its steps are those of "stop", and nothing is asked about a stretch or withheld.

The check samples three points. A limit that acts only strictly between two of them is not seen; the rule assumes that a limit which leaves the knee, the middle and the stop alone leaves the stretch between them alone. A check of each step stands behind that assumption, so that no row is credited with heat its cycles did not deliver and no step under another limit is recorded as reduced-duty charging: the cycles a step on the limit solves anyway – one at the mean of its full-duty stretch, one at the mean of its stretch on the limit – are read as the three qualification cycles are. Each has to deliver the duty the events took there (the nameplate duty, the limit’s duty) to the same 0.1 %, and to name no limit but the condensing one in capacity_binding. A step in which one does not, because it has no operating point or another limit holds it, is withheld the same way, with reduced_duty_withheld == "cycle" where the stretch itself had qualified. That goes for a full-duty cycle that fails outright too: whether the compressor runs in that step, and with which cycle, is then what "stop" resolves (its own solves at its own mean and run time, and at the start of the run where the cycle has no operating point at that mean). None of the charges of the default machines measured here needed it (delivered over asked: 1.000000).

That second check is made at the means of the step’s own stretches, and those move with the step length and with the tank temperature the step starts from. So where the assumption fails – a limit that acts only between the three sampled tank temperatures – the charge depends on the step size: a step is withheld if its cycle lands where that limit acts, and charges on the condensing limit if it lands elsewhere. The qualification is strict about the knee. A compressor that falls short of the nameplate duty there by more than 0.1 % at its speed ceiling withholds the charging ("compressor"), although only the lower end of the stretch is affected. With the default ceiling of 120 rev/s that does not occur for the machines above: the knee cycle runs at 115 rev/s at most (R290 at −12 °C), and in colder air the outdoor coil and the pressure ratio hold it first. It does occur for a compressor given a lower ceiling (rps_max): the 15 kW R290 unit with 100 rev/s is withheld at −5 °C and below (14.8 kW at the knee at −5 °C), with 110 rev/s at −10 °C and below. The reason for the strictness is measured. Below the tank temperature at which the ceiling’s duty meets the condensing limit a step has one duty for its whole full-duty stretch, the one its cycle delivers at that stretch’s mean, while the ceiling’s duty falls as the tank warms. Accepting such a knee, the 15 kW R290 unit with a ceiling of 90 rev/s at −12 and −10 °C outdoor charged the same hour with 17 and 18 % less time on the limit at 600 s than at 60 s steps, and with 1.1 and 1.0 % less electricity at 3600 s (heat within 0.05 %).

Electricity. Along the stretch on the limit the condensing pressure is the limit’s; only the duty changes, from hp_capacity down to Q_min. A step that spends time there solves one more cycle, at the stretch’s mean tank temperature and the limit’s duty at it – which is the stretch’s mean duty exactly, the limit being linear in the tank temperature. That is the midpoint rule in the one variable that varies, as the full-duty stretch is the midpoint rule in the tank temperature. Its error is the curvature of the power over the duty (the power is close to linear in it: 0.33–0.36 W per W from 15 to 5 kW for the R32 unit) times the spread of the duty inside one step. Measured on a whole charge from 50 °C, 3600 s against 60 s steps: −0.11 % (ASHPB R32 at 15 °C outdoor), −0.38 % (the same with a frosting coil at 0 °C), −0.58 % (GSHPB R290), −0.39 % (WSHPB R290). A step that does not reach the cut-out is solved exactly as with "stop". One that does costs one cycle per stretch (two for a step with both, as observed on the four charges above), and the first such step of a source state also pays for the stretch: the cycle at the knee, then the search for the smallest duty (usually three solves; more where a trial the limit refuses is retried half-way back, or where the envelope refuses the probe from below and the smallest duty is itself searched), then the cycles at the middle and at the stop. ASHPB and WSHPB keep the result per source state; GSHPB, whose source is the ground as the step finds it, pays it in every step that gets to the limit. A stretch that fails at the knee costs that one cycle and no search, and its steps then cost what "stop" costs.

The row. A step with both stretches reports the run-time-weighted mean of their two cycles for every rate (heat, power, flow), cop_ref and cop_sys as the ratios of those means, and the state quantities (temperatures, pressures, speed) of the stretch that ran longer (tmhp.dynamic_context.merge_running_rows()). reduced_duty_fraction [-] is the part of the step run on the limit; capacity_clamped, capacity_binding == "condensing_temperature", Q_max [W] (the mean running duty) and Q_request [W] are written as on any reduced step, so the end-of-run summary counts these steps as run below the request. reduced_duty_withheld is None on such a row; a row with reduced-duty charging withheld is the row of "stop" with that key set (Where it applies above).

What it does not cover. Source states in which another limit than the condensing one governs somewhere between the nameplate cut-out and the stop: there the charge ends at the nameplate cut-out, as with "stop", although analyze_steady and a real inverter unit would go on at the duty that other limit leaves. Following that duty inside a step (it is not a straight line in the tank temperature, and it can end abruptly) is left to later work. The multi-node tanks of GSHPB keep the stop: the water around their coil has its own cut-out, at which the compressor already holds it, and the constructor refuses "reduce_duty" with them. With a frosting outdoor coil (ASHPB) the limit binds the frost-free cycle and the delivered heat is its mean over frosting and defrost, still a straight line in the tank temperature; the frost state is the nameplate’s, as on every step. For the ASHPB, analyze_steady itself keeps returning a running point for 0.1 to 1.0 K above the charging limit (R32, 15 kW: 63.3 against 63.1 °C at 7 °C outdoor, 62.2 against 61.3 °C at 35 °C): its duty search finds states at the speed floor that deliver less than the minimum-speed duty by widening the outdoor coil’s approach. The dynamic run stops at the minimum-speed duty analyze_steady reports as Q_min [W]; for GSHPB and WSHPB the two coincide (within the 0.05 K the comparison resolved).

Discharge-temperature control (x_suction_min, default "auto", 0.9 for every refrigerant for now; None switches it off, 1 keeps the superheat stage only) comes first. A running unit whose discharge runs hot opens its expansion valve and, if that is not enough, lets the suction go wet or injects liquid; it does not stop. So when the cycle at a candidate operating point discharges above T_dis_max, the same candidate is solved again, compressor speed included:

  1. with the suction superheat reduced from its solved value – the set dT_superheat capped by the evaporator approach minus dT_hx_min (ASHP, ASHPB, GSHP; GSHPB and WSHPB use the set value) – towards 0 K, to the superheat at which the discharge temperature equals T_dis_max;

  2. if 0 K is not enough, with a two-phase suction of quality x lowered towards x_suction_min, to the quality at which it equals the limit;

  3. if x_suction_min is not enough either, as below.

Wet suction is a homogeneous two-phase state at the evaporating pressure (tmhp.refrigerant.calc_ref_state(), x_suction): the evaporator outlet is two-phase, so the evaporator duty is m_dot (h_1 - h_4) with the wet h_1; the swept mass flow uses the homogeneous density 1 / (x v_g + (1 - x) v_l); the isentropic work is that of the two-phase state. The cycle balance (condenser = evaporator + refrigerant work) closes exactly. The default efficiency forms were fitted to dry-suction data; using them for wet suction is an approximation (its size is discussed below). The quality floor is set per refrigerant (X_SUCTION_MIN), and a number passed as x_suction_min takes precedence over it. Every value is the provisional 0.9 for now, without a quantitative basis and not a reliability limit: two-phase suction is an experimentally used way to hold R32 discharge temperature (Wang et al., ICEC 2014, paper 1595; Zheng et al., IRACC 2016, paper 1766; Seong et al., Appl. Sci. 2017), and the sources collected on #216 put a bound anywhere from quality 0.75 (a Daikin patent’s reliability rating for R32) to 0.84–0.91 (where the refrigerant flow of a 3.5 kW R32 unit collapsed). Values based on that literature are to replace the placeholders (issue #229). Running results report dT_ref_superheat [K], x_ref_cmp_in [-] and discharge_control (bool), the same names in all five families; the invariant suite allows a wet suction only on a controlled row and only down to x_suction_min. Every discharge comparison uses the tolerance DISCHARGE_TOL_K = 0.01 K.

The suction state at the limit is not searched with full cycle solves. At fixed suction and condensing pressure the discharge enthalpy follows from the suction state and the isentropic efficiency alone, so the suction state whose discharge is T_dis_max is first computed from the fluid properties with the efficiency of the state just solved; the cycle is then solved there, and the estimate corrected with the efficiency that solve returned (it moves with the compressor speed, which a denser, wetter suction lowers). Two or three cycle solves close the control to 1e-5 K of the limit, and a point still above the limit at x_suction_min is recognised with one. Root finding on full cycle solves is kept as the fallback for a fluid without a property state or a candidate where a proposed suction state has no cycle.

Known limit: the control itself is an assumption. Neither its order (superheat to 0 K, then suction quality, then a smaller duty), nor stopping at the driest suction state that meets T_dis_max, nor bounding the wet suction by its quality has been confirmed against a manufacturer’s control. One patent describes a similar order (Daikin, US 6,581,397 B1, FIG. 10) without naming the compressor type or the test; units also hold a discharge or discharge-superheat target with the expansion valve, and compressor makers bound liquid return by the oil or shell superheat rather than by the suction quality. Issue #229 is to settle this together with the values.

Known limit: no efficiency penalty for wet suction. A controlled point uses the default efficiency maps unchanged, which is optimistic. Liquid in a compressor’s suction or compression chambers raises the compression power and lowers the compressor efficiency, by an amount that depends on the heat exchanged with the liquid and that is partly recovered in practice through cooler oil and walls (Dutta, Yanagisawa & Fukuta, Int. J. Refrig. 24 (2001) 577–587, for a scroll). No relation between suction quality and isentropic efficiency was found that could be applied to an unspecified compressor without a new input, so none is applied. The default maps do not see a wet suction at all: they take the pressure ratio, the speed and the evaporating temperature, so a wet suction changes the efficiency only through the operating point it leads to.

The table has not been measured again with the present maps. With them the control acts more widely: the same boiler delivers its full 15 kW at A−7 and A−10 against a 55 °C tank and at A−15 against a 50 °C tank, with suction qualities of 0.96, 0.94 and 0.93. A penalty would therefore act on points that run at full duty (an inference; not measured).

Discharge control acts on 22 of 153 air-to-water catalogue points. It reduces superheat alone on 5 points, including 2 with R410A. The 17 wet-suction points all use R32, with suction quality from 0.934 to 0.995. One wet-suction point falls short of its published duty and is excluded from the COP score. The other 16 average +0.4 % COP bias against the catalogue. The 5 points with reduced superheat average −5.9 %, and the 124 scored points without control average −6.7 %. These groups cover different operating conditions and units, so their biases describe agreement with the catalogues at those points. See Validation (Capacity) for the errors by control state.

Real units can limit discharge temperature by reducing suction superheat, using two-phase suction or injection where supported, and reducing compressor speed. For example, Panasonic lists 8.7 kW at −15 °C outdoors and 60 °C leaving water for its nominal 12 kW WH-MXC12J9E8. The model meets that published duty with wet suction. The wet-suction stage stays enabled by default. Its quality floor is a model input for a controller whose suction state is unspecified.

How each family answers a point the control cannot hold:

  • ASHP, GSHP — the candidate is rejected inside the operating-point search, like a pressure-ratio ceiling. The search moves to a smaller lift if one carries the duty; otherwise the capacity search returns the largest deliverable duty with capacity_clamped == "max" and capacity_binding == "discharge_temperature". With no deliverable duty the result is the off state with failure_reason == "discharge_temp_above_max" – unless (ASHP) the request is only too small: refused at low speed, where the efficiency falls and the discharge runs hotter, while a larger duty runs. It is then met by on/off cycling at the smallest duty that runs (capacity_clamped == "min"), as described for the boilers below.

    ASHP decides both questions at the least-lift point of a duty — both coils at their largest airflow, the smallest pressure ratio and the coolest discharge at which the duty is delivered (AirSourceHeatPump._largest_airflow_point). It is solved as a fixed point of the two approach temperatures and is one of the starting points of the least-power search, so a band of running approaches thinner than any lattice is still entered (about 1 K wide for the default unit at −25 °C). A duty whose least-lift point is outside the envelope has no operating point; the unit then runs at the largest duty below the request whose least-lift point is inside (bisected to 0.1 %), capacity_binding naming the limit the next larger duty runs into — also when a still larger duty would run again, which happens because at mid speed the compressor is less efficient and its discharge hotter than flat out. As for ASHPB below, the control also acts on a candidate whose coils do not carry the cycle, so the coil margins the search reads are those of the controlled cycle.

  • ASHPB outdoor-approach search — a candidate outside the envelope (after the control) ranks after every solved candidate, including one short of the request at rps_max, and before an unphysical state, a coil that cannot carry the duty and no cycle; within that tier it is graded by how far outside it is at the control’s last stage (the suction at x_suction_min), so the search moves to the smaller lift that may be inside. The control also acts on a candidate whose coil does not carry the cycle, so the band of approaches the fan can serve is that of the controlled cycle: wet suction adds to the evaporator duty (about 100 W of 15 kW), which moves the band’s lower edge up by a few hundredths of a kelvin, and above the band the control runs out at x_suction_min. Read from the uncontrolled cycle, the band left a window of running approaches that the scan could step over (a request of 15 kW at A-6/W50 was then “reduced” to 14.7 kW, and to 8.4 kW one step earlier).

  • ASHPB, GSHPB, WSHPB (analyze_steady) — the solved operating point is checked after tmhp.dynamic_context.unphysical_cycle_detail(). A request with no operating point for a reason a smaller duty cures – the discharge limit, the pressure-ratio ceiling, the source heat exchanger’s range (tmhp.compressor_envelope.duty_reduction_binding()) – is retried at 80/60/40/25/10 % and the boundary is bisected to 1 % of the request; the largest duty that runs is returned with capacity_clamped == "max", capacity_binding naming the limit active at that point – the pressure-ratio ceiling, then the speed ceiling rps_max ("compressor", when the returned point already runs at it), then the limit the next larger duty ran into (compressor_envelope._reduced_binding; see failure_reason semantics), Q_max [W] and Q_request [W]. If none runs the off state carries the request’s own reason – except a request refused from below, by the discharge limit, which is then tried at larger duties (tmhp.compressor_envelope.raise_duty_within_envelope(): 5 to 200 % of the nameplate from the bottom, the boundary bisected to 0.5 %). At a large lift the compressor efficiency falls towards low speed: the discharge runs hotter there, past T_dis_max even at x_suction_min. The smallest duty that runs is then the lower end of continuous operation, returned with capacity_clamped == "min" and averaged over the on/off period like a minimum-speed row (Q_min [W] is that duty; the loss is the EN 14825 degradation). The climb passes the rungs refused for the discharge limit and ends at the first that is not; if that rung is refused as well, by a limit of the other side (the pressure ratio, the heat exchanger, the condensing temperature), the duties that run lie between it and the rung below and the bisection goes by which side refuses the trial (the 15 kW R32 boiler at A−20 against a 60 °C tank: 2.1 to 4.3 kW, between the discharge limit below and the pressure-ratio ceiling above). The request keeps its reason when no duty runs: the two sides meet (the same boiler at A−25 against a 55 °C tank: the discharge limit refuses the smallest duties and the pressure-ratio ceiling every larger one). Of the 2026-11-15 sweep (5,796 boiler points, with the efficiency map of then), 73 requests refused for the discharge temperature while a larger one ran became cycling rows, at a Q_min of 15–84 % of the nameplate (median 38 %); the 3,803 rows that ran before were unchanged. The limits are not monotone in the duty together: above the duties that run the pressure ratio or the heat exchanger refuses, below them the discharge temperature does (at low speed the efficiency falls). So when no rung runs, two neighbouring rungs refused for different reasons are bisected by reason, which finds a running range narrower than the ladder spacing (with the efficiency map of before November 2026, R1234yf A-15/W65: 4.0–5.7 kW of a 15 kW request; with the present defaults that request runs at 3.3 kW, held by the pressure ratio). Three short cuts keep “no duty runs” cheap: a request refused by the pressure ratio, or by a coil short at full airflow, has its 10 % rung solved first, and if that is refused the same way nothing in between is tried (those limits only tighten with the duty); a rung at which every approach has the compressor on its speed floor ends the ladder (smaller requests are the same cycles); and a request with no cycle at all is not reduced.

  • Boiler dynamic steps answer the same way. Each step solves its cycle once (at the mean tank temperature of its run, see tmhp.dynamic_context.resolve_step_operation()): held by the control if possible, otherwise at the largest duty that runs, whose heat then sets the run time and the step-average rates (the tank reaches its upper bound later); a step with no duty that runs is an off step with the reason. The start-temperature retry of that function applies the same reduction. A step that needs the reduction costs one cycle solve per ladder rung and bisection point: with the efficiency map of before November 2026, 24 h of the default R32 boiler on a cold day (outdoor −8 to 0 °C, dt 600 s; 10 of 22 running steps at a reduced duty, 70 called steps with no duty that runs) took about half a minute, against one to two seconds on a mild day where the control alone held the limit.

Steady results of every family carry efficiency_extrapolated (bool): the point lies outside the data of a default efficiency map (30–110 rev/s and the hull in pressure ratio and evaporating temperature). Scalar and user efficiencies give False.

Until November 2026 the envelope had a third limit, an isentropic-efficiency floor (ETA_ISEN_MIN = 0.30, failure_reason == "eta_isen_below_min", capacity_binding == "compressor_efficiency"): the BITZER quadratic map, read outside its data towards low speed, fell to 0.14, and points at which it was extrapolated and below 0.30 were refused. The floor, its failure reason and its binding are removed. The maps that ship now are polynomials with no floor of their own: a nonpositive value makes the point infeasible by the range rule, and the lower side of the envelope is the discharge-temperature limit alone.
