Air-source heat pump (ASHP — space conditioning)¶

ASHP conditions a building zone (heating + cooling) rather than charging a DHW tank. The refrigerant cycle and outdoor-coil source side are shared with the air-source boiler family; what differs is the demand side — a zone energy balance instead of a tank.

Overview¶

The class is tmhp.AirSourceHeatPump. Use it when the heat pump’s job is space conditioning rather than DHW production.

Base usage¶

from tmhp import AirSourceHeatPump

ashp = AirSourceHeatPump(ref="R32")

# See API reference below for the full constructor and
# analyze_steady / analyze_dynamic signatures.

Source-side mechanics¶

Outdoor coil with variable-speed fan and an ε-NTU air-side heat exchanger — the shared air-source environmental-side model.

Air density¶

A fan moves a volume of air and a coil exchanges heat with a mass of it. Both coils take the density of the air they draw in — outdoor air at T0 for the outdoor coil, room air at T_a_room for the indoor coil — as dry air at one standard atmosphere (tmhp.air_properties.air_density()):

The airflows the model reports and accepts (dV_ou_a, dV_iu_a, the fan references and limits) are volumes at the coil inlet, so the air mass flow of a coil is air_density(T_in) * dV and its heat capacity rate c_a * air_density(T_in) * dV. That one mass flow carries the coil’s NTU, the outlet temperature and the fan heat, so within a row

c_a * rho(T_in) * dV * (T_out - T_in) = heat from the refrigerant + fan power

holds on each coil (Q_ref_ou, E_ou_fan on the outdoor side; Q_ref_iu, E_iu_fan on the indoor side; the refrigerant heat is negative where the coil is the evaporator). Q_a_iu_in / Q_a_iu_out and the air exergy flows use the same mass flow. A row averaged over on/off cycling or frost keeps the identity on the indoor side; on the outdoor side the fan power is averaged with the runtime fraction and the airflow with the capacity ratio, so the two sides differ by that share of the fan power.

At a fixed fan volume flow, cold outdoor air therefore carries more heat capacity than the 15 °C standard air earlier releases assumed at every temperature (1.225 kg/m³): 16 % more at −25 °C, 6.5 % less at 35 °C. Room air is 2 to 5 % lighter than that standard air (20 to 30 °C). For the default unit against those releases:

  • Heating. The outdoor coil gains air mass in the cold (the evaporating temperature is 0.1 K higher below 0 °C) and the indoor coil loses 1.7 % at a 20 °C room (the condensing temperature is 0.1 to 0.2 K higher). The two nearly cancel: the COP is 0.3 % lower at 7 °C / 20 °C at nameplate capacity and within 0.2 % at half load down to −15 °C.

  • Cooling. Both coils move warm, lighter air. At 35 °C outdoors and a 27 °C room the evaporating temperature at nameplate capacity is 0.5 K lower and the COP 1.9 % lower (1.3 % at half load); at a 30 °C room the COP at nameplate capacity is up to 4 % lower.

  • Ceiling. The largest duty the indoor coil carries inside the approach bounds (dT_approach_bounds) falls with the room-air density, by 1.3 % at a 20 °C room. Where that ceiling is close to the request it now binds: at −7 °C / 20 °C the frosted unit delivers 98.8 % of its nameplate (capacity_clamped == "max") where it delivered 100 %.

The volume flows are the fan’s actual flows at the coil inlet. Rating airflows are commonly quoted as standard air (1.204 kg/m³, the mass flow divided by that density); at 20 °C the two are the same number, at a 27 °C room the actual flow is 2.4 % larger and at 35 °C outdoors 5 % larger. Pass the actual flow when the difference matters.

What stays on the volume flow: the coil conductance follows the fan’s volume flow (UA_rated * (dV / dV_ref) ** n), and the fan power follows its curve in the volume flow at the rated static pressure. The fan laws give a pressure rise, and so a fan power, proportional to the air density at a fixed volume flow; that is not applied. Applied, it would add 16 % to the outdoor fan’s power at −25 °C, which is 0.5 to 0.7 % of the total input (1.2 % at most), and take 0.3 to 0.4 % off the total input at 35 °C. Humidity is left out — per cubic metre moist air carries 0.2 to 0.4 % more sensible heat capacity than dry air at room and summer conditions — and so is altitude (one standard atmosphere; 18 % less density at 1 600 m).

Sink-side mechanics¶

A zone temperature / load proxy stands in for the building. The caller supplies indoor-unit load as Q_r_iu: positive values select cooling, negative values select heating, and zero values represent off operation. There is no tank energy balance.

Indoor airflow¶

The rated indoor airflow defaults to 5.37e-5 m³/s per watt of hp_capacity — 400 cfm per ton, 193 m³/h per kW, the airflow at which unitary equipment is rated (tmhp.hx_fan.INDOOR_AIRFLOW_PER_W). Pass dV_iu_fan_a_rated when the unit’s own figure is known; wall-mounted split units publish 170 to 310 m³/h per kW at their high fan speed.

The airflow decides the supply-air temperature and, through the indoor coil’s approach, most of the temperature lift. At the default a unit delivering its nameplate capacity supplies air about 15 K from the room: 35 °C in heating at a 20 °C room, 12 °C in cooling at a 27 °C room, with the refrigerant saturated 17 K from the room air. (The earlier default, 720 m³/h per kW, moved four times that air: supply air 5 K from the room, a condensing temperature near 30 °C, and a rated COP above what such equipment declares.) The indoor fan draws about 0.5 % of the nameplate capacity at rated flow, both fans together 1.3 to 2.5 %.

Because the fan power is small against the compressor’s, the least-power operating point keeps the indoor fan at its largest airflow at every load; the supply-air temperature therefore approaches the room temperature at part load. A unit that slows its fan for comfort is described with dV_iu_fan_a_max.

Note

The approach-temperature search domain (dT_approach_bounds, 1–20 K) is close to the 17 K the indoor coil runs at nameplate capacity, so in mild weather it caps the delivered heat near 120 % of the nameplate (capacity_clamped == "max", capacity_binding == "cycle"). That cap is the search box, not the machine: widen the bounds — the validation harness uses (1, 30) — when larger duties are to be explored.

Part load: cycling and unmet load¶

The compressor modulates between rps_min and rps_max. A request outside what the unit delivers over that range is still answered, and the row says how.

Below the minimum capacity the compressor cycles on and off, and the row is the mean over the cycling period (capacity_clamped == "min"):

  • Q_ref_iu [W] equals the request. Q_min [W] is what the unit delivers running continuously at its minimum speed, and capacity_ratio [-] is the request divided by it (the CR of EN 14825).

  • The electrical input carries the EN 14825 degradation for a unit delivering to air: COP = COP_min · (1 − Cd · (1 − CR)) with Cd = 0.25, the value the standard prescribes when a unit has not been tested for it. part_load_factor [-] is that bracket. It applies to the refrigeration machine — compressor and outdoor fan — so cop_ref [-] is the continuous value times it, and cop_sys [-] falls slightly less (about one percent) because the indoor fan is not degraded.

  • runtime_fraction [-] is the share of the time the compressor runs, CR / part_load_factor. Compressor and outdoor-fan power (E_cmp, E_ou_fan) are the running values times that fraction. Heat and flow rates (Q_ref_iu, Q_ref_ou, Q_a_iu_in, Q_a_iu_out, m_dot_ref, dV_iu_a, dV_ou_a) are the running values times CR, and so is the indoor fan’s power (E_iu_fan): it runs while heat is delivered. The refrigerant cycle and both air streams thus stay in balance with the delivered heat, and the room still receives the coil duty plus the indoor fan’s heat. Temperatures, pressures, compressor speed and efficiencies are those of the running machine.

  • E_cycling_loss [W] is the mean electrical power the compressor and the outdoor fan draw beyond CR times their continuous power — the cost of the starts.

A request of a tenth of the minimum capacity is thus met at 77.5 % of the continuous minimum-speed COP, with the compressor running 12.9 % of the time. Without this model every request below the floor was answered with the minimum capacity itself — about half the nameplate for the default unit in mild weather — whatever was asked for. Cd_cycling sets the coefficient: a number replaces 0.25, 0 is loss-free cycling, and None restores the earlier behaviour (continuous operation at the minimum speed, Q_ref_iu [W] above the request, runtime_fraction [-] of 1). The operating-point search weighs the degradation, so the minimum-speed point may sit a few watts from the one found with Cd_cycling=None.

Above the maximum capacity the unit runs flat out (capacity_clamped == "max"): Q_ref_iu [W] is the ceiling, Q_max [W] repeats it, capacity_binding names what set it and Q_unmet [W] is the part of the request that is not delivered. Q_request [W] (signed, as asked) and Q_unmet [W] are on every row, so a schedule’s unmet energy is the sum of Q_unmet [W] times the time step.

The ceiling is the unit running flat out: the compressor at rps_max and both fans at their largest airflow. A coil carries a duty with the smallest temperature difference at its largest airflow, so this is the least lift — the smallest pressure ratio and the coolest discharge — at which that speed’s duty is delivered; if this point is outside the compressor envelope, so is every other approach pair delivering the duty. It is solved directly (both approach temperatures as a fixed point, about twenty cycle evaluations), not searched for. Where it is outside the envelope, or beyond what a coil carries inside the approach bounds, a unit’s controller holds the compressor back, and the ceiling is the largest duty whose least-lift point is an operating point, bisected to 0.1 %.

The same holds below the ceiling. The envelope is not monotone in the duty: at mid speed the compressor is less efficient and its discharge hotter than flat out, so a duty can be refused although a larger one runs (default unit, −25 °C outdoor / 24 °C room: 2.3 kW has no operating point, 3.6 kW has). Such a request runs at the largest duty below it that has one, again with capacity_clamped == "max" and the binding that names the limit, as the water-heating models do. A request is switched off (failure_reason) only when no smaller duty runs either, and the reason is then the limit its own least-lift point breaks — not the one an arbitrary probe of the search happened to break.

Note

The ceiling is not the largest heat any pair of approach temperatures delivers at rps_max. An adiabatic compressor turns its losses into delivered heat, so starving a coil of air (a higher pressure ratio, a lower isentropic efficiency) can raise the heating duty by up to a tenth while the COP falls by up to a quarter; no unit runs its fans that way. Earlier versions ranked capacity-limited candidates by delivered heat and returned such states — the default R290 unit at −15 °C / 18 °C with the outdoor fan at 18 % of its airflow and a COP of 1.9 against 2.8.

What the cycling model does not contain: the indoor fan is taken to run only while heat is delivered, no standby or crankcase power is added while the unit is off, and a start is not resolved in time — the degradation is the standard’s seasonal-average figure, not a transient simulation.

Frost and defrost¶

In heating the outdoor coil runs below the outdoor air. Below about 5 °C it is below freezing and colder than the air’s dew point, so frost begins to build on it, capacity and power fall, and the unit periodically stops heating to melt the frost. A catalogue figure at 2 °C or −7 °C is the mean over that; the frost-free cycle the model solves is not.

AirSourceHeatPump therefore applies the frost adjustment EnergyPlus uses for its DX heating coils (Coil:Heating:DX:SingleSpeed, on-demand reverse-cycle defrost; from DOE-2.1E), with its published constants unchanged (tmhp.defrost):

\[T_{coil} = 0.82\,T_{out} - 8.589, \qquad \Delta\omega = \max\left(10^{-6},\ \omega_{out} - \omega_{sat}(T_{coil})\right), \qquad t_{frac} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.01446/\Delta\omega}\]
\[\text{capacity multiplier} = 0.875\,(1 - t_{frac}), \qquad \text{power multiplier} = 0.954\,(1 - t_{frac})\]

and no correction above an outdoor temperature of 5 °C. EnergyPlus switches the whole correction on at that limit; here it grows from nothing at 5 °C to its full size at 2 °C, the frosting rating condition of EN 14511 (A2), as the share \(w\) of the runtime the coil frosts:

\[w = \min\left(1,\ \frac{5 - T_{out}}{3}\right), \qquad t_{frac} \leftarrow w\,t_{frac}, \qquad \text{multiplier} \leftarrow 1 - w\,(1 - \text{multiplier})\]

— the mean of a unit that frosts for that share and runs frost-free for the rest. At and below 2 °C and above 5 °C nothing differs from EnergyPlus. A request Q has to be met by the mean, so the cycle is solved frost-free at (Q + Q_defrost) / capacity multiplier and the row is then averaged:

  • Q_ref_iu [W] is the mean delivered heat — the request, unless the larger frost-free duty runs into the capacity ceiling, in which case the shortfall is in Q_unmet [W]. Frost lowers the ceiling by the capacity multiplier.

  • defrost_fraction [-] is t_frac; frost_capacity_factor [-] and frost_power_factor [-] are the two multipliers.

  • E_cmp [W] is the frost-free compressor power times power multiplier + t_frac (heating, then defrosting); E_defrost [W] is the defrosting part. The outdoor fan follows the power multiplier, and the indoor fan’s power is averaged with the heat it delivers.

  • Q_defrost [W] is the heat a reverse-cycle defrost takes from the room (0.01 · t_frac · (7.222 − T_out) · hp_capacity / 1.01667).

  • Q_ref_iu_frost_free [W] and E_tot_frost_free [W] are the frost-free cycle the row was solved at; heat and flow rates of the row are that cycle’s times the delivered-heat factor, and state points are that cycle’s.

The outdoor humidity is an optional input: analyze_steady(..., RH_out=0.7) and analyze_dynamic(..., RH_out_schedule=...) take a relative humidity as a fraction. Without it the model uses 84 %, the humidity of the EN 14511 rating condition A2 (dry bulb 2 °C, wet bulb 1 °C). defrost=None switches the correction off, and a tmhp.DefrostModel sets the outdoor limit, the width of the transition below it and the default humidity.

Three things differ from EnergyPlus. The transition above replaces a switch: in EnergyPlus the correction is in full at 5.0 °C and absent at 5.1 °C, where its cost is at its largest, so the COP of the default unit stepped from 3.49 to 4.64 between 5.0 and 5.5 °C. The 5 °C limit is a setting of the defrost controller, not the temperature at which a coil frosts over, and a coil near freezing frosts on part of its surface and for part of the time. DefrostModel(transition_K=0) restores the switch. The other two are there so that nothing has to be entered: EnergyPlus asks for a curve of defrost power, and here the compressor draws, while it defrosts, what it draws while it heats; and the coil temperature is EnergyPlus’ correlation rather than the evaporating temperature this model solves — the constants were fitted with that estimate.

What it leaves out: dry air is not frost-free in the source model (the frosted-coil multipliers 0.875 and 0.954 apply whenever the outdoor air is below the limit, whatever its humidity), and that is kept; and the room is not cooled in time during a defrost — its effect is in the mean. max_capacity() reports the same mean ceiling, with the frost-free one as Q_max_frost_free [W].

COP boundary¶

Q_r_iu is the coil requirement before fan heat, not net room delivery. It retains its sign convention and governs compressor speed, rated capacity, part-load ratio and the operating-point search. cop_ref [-] remains Q_ref_iu [W] / E_cmp [W].

The space-conditioning system metric is abs(Q_a_iu_out [W] - Q_a_iu_in [W]) / E_tot [W]. Both air energy flows use the actual indoor airflow and the same outdoor reference temperature. The final outlet already includes indoor fan heat: net cooling equals coil duty minus indoor fan power, while net heating equals coil duty plus that power. Do not add or subtract it a second time. The denominator includes compressor, indoor fan and outdoor fan electricity.

indoor_heat_direction_valid identifies delivery in the requested mode. Opposite-direction transfer or zero airflow during active operation gives NaN system COP; off operation has zero air energy flows and NaN COP. This reporting diagnostic does not alter the coil-load solver.

This dry-coil model reports sensible cooling, not total latent-plus-sensible cooling or a certified rating. Catalogue comparisons must use the catalogue’s own capacity and electrical-input boundary. Earlier results through commit c5d673e used Q_ref_iu / E_tot and must be labelled as coil-duty COP. For a seasonal performance factor, sum actual mode-specific air delivery times timestep duration and divide by summed system electricity times duration; an arithmetic mean of hourly COP is a different statistic.

API reference¶

Air source heat pump — physics-based cycle model with indoor unit.

Resolves a vapour-compression refrigerant cycle coupled to an outdoor-air heat exchanger and an indoor-air heat exchanger. Supports both cooling (Q_r_iu > 0) and heating (Q_r_iu < 0) modes. The indoor load Q_r_iu is imposed externally each timestep.

At each time step the model finds the minimum-power operating point (compressor + indoor fan + outdoor fan) via bounded 2-D optimisation over the evaporator and condenser approach temperature differences.

Architecture mirrors AirSourceHeatPumpBoiler — uses the same shared utility functions (calc_ref_state, calc_HX_perf_for_target_heat, calc_fan_power_from_dV_fan) and the same postprocess_exergy() pattern, but replaces the tank energy balance with direct air-side heat exchange at the indoor unit.

tmhp.air_source_heat_pump.OBJ_CAPACITY_LIMITED = 1000000000.0¶

Objective floor for a capacity-limited operating point (valid operation).

tmhp.air_source_heat_pump.OBJ_INFEASIBLE = 1000000000000.0¶

Objective value for an operating point the cycle cannot reach (invalid).

tmhp.air_source_heat_pump.NARROW_FAN_FLOW_RATIO = 2.0¶

A fan whose largest flow is at most this multiple of its smallest is searched by _NarrowFanSearch. At a fixed approach temperature a coil’s capacity grows roughly as flow^0.7, so a flow ratio of 2 leaves a band of approach temperatures about +/-25 % wide – at a 3 K approach that is narrower than the spacing of the finest lattice the grid search falls back on (1.3 K), and a constant-speed fan leaves a single point.

Air source heat pump with indoor-unit air heat exchange.

The refrigerant cycle is resolved via CoolProp with user-specified superheat / subcool margins. A bounded 2-D optimiser minimises total electrical input (E_cmp + E_iu_fan + E_ou_fan) over the evaporator and condenser approach temperatures.

When the requested duty has no operating point – the compressor runs out of speed, or the compressor envelope or a coil binds first – the unit runs at its ceiling: flat out (rps_max, both fans at their largest airflow), or at the largest duty the envelope admits (max_capacity()). Such an hour is reported with capacity_clamped == "max", a Q_ref_iu [W] below the request and capacity_binding naming the limit; the difference is unmet load (Q_unmet [W]), not a solver failure.

When the request is below what the unit delivers at its minimum compressor speed, the compressor cycles on and off. Such an hour is reported with capacity_clamped == "min" as the mean over the cycling period: the delivered heat equals the request, the electrical input carries the EN 14825 degradation (Cd_cycling), and runtime_fraction [-] is the share of the time the compressor runs (tmhp.cycling).

max_capacity(T0, T_a_room=None, *, mode='heating', grid_points=None, rel_tol=0.001, RH_out=None)[source]¶

Largest duty the unit can actually deliver at this condition.

Nameplate capacity is a label attached to one rating point, not a ceiling. A real unit delivers more than nameplate when the outdoor air is mild and less when it is severe, because the limit is set by whichever of the compressor speed range, the compressor envelope and the air-side heat exchangers binds first. Clipping a load schedule at nameplate therefore errs in both directions at once. This returns the ceiling the model itself implies, so a schedule can be clipped against the machine rather than against its label and the shortfall reported as a result.

The ceiling is the unit running flat out: the compressor at rps_max and both fans at their largest airflow, which is the least lift at which that speed’s duty is delivered (_largest_airflow_point()). Where that point is outside the compressor envelope (pressure ratio, discharge temperature even under its control, the efficiency floor) or beyond what a coil carries inside the approach bounds, the ceiling is the largest duty whose least-lift point is an operating point – a unit’s controller holds the compressor back to it – found by bisection on the duty.

The ceiling is not the largest heat any approach pair delivers at rps_max. An adiabatic compressor turns its losses into delivered heat, so starving the outdoor coil of air (a lower evaporating pressure, a higher pressure ratio, a lower isentropic efficiency) can raise the heating duty a little while the COP collapses; no unit runs its fans that way.

Warning

The answer is only as trustworthy as the compressor correlations at the speeds it reaches. Those correlations are fits, and a validation carried out at part load does not cover the top of the speed range. Check that the returned cop_sys [-] is credible before using the ceiling, and consider lowering rps_max to the highest speed the validation actually reached.

Parameters:

  • T0 (float) – Outdoor air temperature [°C].

  • T_a_room (float | None) – Room air temperature [°C]. Uses the constructor default if None.

  • mode (str) – "heating" or "cooling".

  • grid_points (int | None) – Unused. The ceiling was once swept on a grid of approach temperatures of this resolution; it is solved now. Accepted so that existing calls keep running.

  • rel_tol (float) – Relative width at which the bisection on the duty stops when the unit cannot run flat out. A flat-out ceiling and one set by the indoor coil are exact.

  • RH_out (float | None) – Outdoor relative humidity as a fraction (0-1) for the frost correction in heating. None: the frost model’s default.

Returns:

Q_max [W] (0.0 when the unit cannot run at all here), the approach temperatures there, the electrical input and system COP at that point, and binding naming what stopped it: "compressor" (the speed range), "pressure_ratio", "discharge_temperature" or "compressor_efficiency" (the compressor envelope: the next larger duty exceeds PR_cycle_max, T_dis_max under its control, or falls below the efficiency floor), "cycle" (a coil at its largest airflow and the widest approach the search admits), "cycle_invalid" (the next larger duty has no cycle the model can represent – a condensing temperature at the critical point; analyze_steady() does not settle on that edge) and "infeasible" when no duty has an operating point.

In heating with a frosting outdoor coil the ceiling, the input and the COP are the mean over frosting and defrost – what analyze_steady() delivers there – and Q_max_frost_free [W] is the frost-free ceiling they come from (defrost_fraction [-] > 0). Otherwise the two are equal.

Return type:

dict

analyze_steady(Q_r_iu, T0, T_a_room=None, *, return_dict=True, postprocess=True, verbose=True, RH_out=None)[source]¶

Run a steady-state performance snapshot.

Parameters:

  • Q_r_iu (float) – Indoor thermal load [W]. >0 cooling, <0 heating, 0 off.

  • T0 (float) – Dead-state / outdoor-air temperature [°C].

  • T_a_room (float | None) – Room air temperature [°C]. Uses constructor default if None.

  • return_dict (bool) – If True return dict; else single-row DataFrame.

  • postprocess (bool) – If True, apply postprocess_exergy to the output.

  • verbose (bool) – If True, print warnings upon convergence failure.

  • RH_out (float | None) – Outdoor relative humidity as a fraction (0-1), used by the frost correction in heating. None: the frost model’s default (0.84).

Returns:

Cycle state plus diagnostic flags.

Two keys are useful for branching:

  • "converged" (bool) — True only when the inner HX optimisation and the SciPy optimiser both succeeded.

  • "failure_reason" (str) — one of "none", "cycle_invalid" (the refrigerant cycle itself was infeasible), "pr_above_max", "discharge_temp_above_max", "condensing_temp_above_max", "eta_isen_below_min" (the compressor envelope refuses the request where it is easiest to meet – at its least-lift point – and no smaller duty runs either; a request that a smaller duty meets returns that duty with capacity_clamped="max" and capacity_binding naming the limit: "discharge_temperature", "condensing_temperature" for T_cond_max, "compressor_efficiency"), "hx_not_converged" (cycle OK but a coil cannot carry the duty inside the approach bounds), or "optimizer_failed" (SciPy reported success=False).

ASHP triggers an off-mode fallback for any of the non-"none" reasons — E_cmp [W] will be 0 and the COP keys will be NaN in that case. Treat failure_reason != "none" as “do not trust the numbers”.

Part load is reported on every row: Q_request [W] (signed, as asked), Q_unmet [W] (request not delivered, >= 0), runtime_fraction [-], capacity_ratio [-], part_load_factor [-], Q_min [W] and E_cycling_loss [W]. A request below the minimum continuous capacity (capacity_clamped == "min") is the mean over an on/off cycling period: Q_ref_iu [W] equals the request, heat and flow rates are the running values times capacity_ratio [-] (the indoor fan’s power with them), compressor and outdoor-fan power the running values times runtime_fraction [-], and the COP keys carry the EN 14825 degradation; temperatures, pressures and speed are those of the running machine (tmhp.cycling). A request above the ceiling (capacity_clamped == "max") adds Q_max [W] and capacity_binding ("compressor", "discharge_temperature", "pressure_ratio", "compressor_efficiency" or "cycle"; see max_capacity()).

Heating below the frost model’s outdoor limit (5 degC; the correction grows to its full size at 2 degC) is the mean over frosting and defrost (tmhp.defrost): defrost_fraction [-] is the share of the runtime spent defrosting, frost_capacity_factor [-] and frost_power_factor [-] the multipliers of the frosted coil, E_defrost [W] and Q_defrost [W] the mean power and room heat the defrost takes, and Q_ref_iu_frost_free [W] / E_tot_frost_free [W] the frost-free cycle the row was solved at. Without frost those two repeat Q_ref_iu [W] and E_tot [W].

Return type:

dict | DataFrame
