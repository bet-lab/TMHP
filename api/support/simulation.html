tmhp.dynamic_context.TEMPERATURE_TOL_K: float = 1e-06

Temperature width [K] within which the tank counts as at a thermostat bound. The tank temperature at a step boundary comes from an implicit root solve, so a tank that ends a step on a bound lands a few 1e-9 K on either side of it depending on the platform’s floating-point path. An exact comparison then turns the next step’s call – and with it hp_is_on and hp_starts [-] – on or off by machine. 1e-6 K is far above that noise and far below anything a thermostat resolves.

tmhp.dynamic_context.bound_tolerance(T_lower, T_upper)[source]

Width [K] for the bound comparisons of a [T_lower, T_upper] band.

TEMPERATURE_TOL_K, but at most a quarter of the band, so the “at the lower bound” and “at the upper bound” tests never overlap: with an overlap the lower test, checked first, would keep the call on at the upper bound and the compressor would run past it. A degenerate band (T_lower >= T_upper) gets no width (exact comparisons).

class tmhp.dynamic_context.Subsystem(*args, **kwargs)[source]

Bases: Protocol

Pluggable subsystem interface.

Each subsystem computes its contribution for a single timestep and assembles result columns for the output DataFrame. New subsystems (PV, battery, …) implement this protocol and register with the boiler model.

Carried state¶

A subsystem with memory between steps (a controller latch, a lock-out) does not keep it on its own instance: it is part of the run and travels in DynamicState.subsystem_state[name].

  • Read: during a step the host model holds the previous step’s states in host._subsystem_state_in ({} at the start of a run); the subsystem’s entry is host._subsystem_state_in.get(name, {}). It must not be modified. The host’s hooks (_run_subsystems, _augment_results) read it there; a subsystem object stepped through this protocol has no reference to its host, so the hook that calls it hands the entry over.

  • Write: the subsystem puts its next state, a new plain dict, under SUBSYSTEM_STATE_KEY ("carried_state") of the dict its step() returned, at the latest in assemble_results() (the state may depend on the solved tank temperature). The host gathers these with collect_subsystem_state().

A step()-driven host (ASHPB) threads the states through the DynamicState it returns; a host that only offers analyze_dynamic (GSHPB) keeps them for the run and clears them when a run starts. Either way a second run with the same inputs starts from empty state.

Determine refill flow rate from current level and operational mode.

Pure tank-level management: all subsystem-specific flow overrides (e.g. STC mains-preheat forced refill) are the responsibility of the scenario class via _run_subsystems / ctrl.dV_tank_w_in_ctrl.

Parameters:

  • tank_level (float) – Current fractional tank level (0–1).

  • dV_tank_w_out (float) – Current outflow rate [m³/s].

  • V_tank_full (float) – Tank full volume [m³].

  • tank_always_full (bool) – Whether the tank is forced to stay full.

  • prevent_simultaneous_flow (bool) – Exclusive-flow mode flag.

  • tank_level_lower_bound (float) – Level lower bound for refill trigger.

  • tank_level_upper_bound (float) – Level upper bound for refill cut-off.

  • dV_tank_w_in_refill (float) – Refill flow rate [m³/s].

  • is_refilling (bool) – Whether we are currently in a refill cycle.

Return type:

tuple[float | None, bool]

Notes

dV_tank_w_out is the draw expected over the step. The returned make-up is a step-average rate:

  • tank_always_full=False: the refill valve is followed through the step (_refill_within_step()). It opens when the falling level reaches tank_level_lower_bound and closes when it is back at tank_level_upper_bound, at any point of the step, and admits dV_tank_w_in_refill while open.

Either way a step whose full-rate refill would overshoot the target takes the part that reaches it. Compressor operation in one step and the cycle it ran.

residual_free(T_K) is the boiler’s own implicit tank residual built with a zero heat-source rate; it supplies the heat the tank gains or loses from everything else (tank_free_heat_rate()), linearised between min(T_start, T_lower) and max(T_start, T_upper).   1. the thermostat events with the compressor delivering Q_request, which gives when it runs and the mean tank temperature while it does;

tmhp.dynamic_context.unphysical_cycle_detail(result, hp_capacity)[source]

Explain why a cycle state the solver accepted is not an operating point.

The boiler solvers accept a cycle once its compressor speed meets the requested duty (or clamps onto a speed bound). That acceptance says nothing about whether the state itself exists. This check rejects the states that cannot be a running machine; None means the state passes.

"duty_or_power_undefined"

Delivered heat or compressor power is non-finite or not positive. A running compressor does work on the refrigerant and the condenser rejects at least that work, so both must be positive numbers.

"discharge_state_undefined"

The compressor discharge temperature or enthalpy is non-finite. The delivered heat is m_dot * (h_discharge - h_liquid); without a discharge state that heat has no thermodynamic basis. A low isentropic efficiency at a high pressure ratio produces it: h_isen - h_suction is divided by the efficiency and the discharge enthalpy leaves the refrigerant’s equation-of-state range (with the guarded default fit, R32 at PR 7-11 and eta_is 0.14-0.25; with an unguarded fit, eta_is near zero).

"min_speed_duty_above_capacity"

The compressor clamped onto its speed floor (the request was below the duty available there) and yet delivers more than the nameplate hp_capacity. Mass flow is proportional to speed, so at rps_min (below the speed at which hp_capacity is rated) the compressor pumps the least refrigerant it can; delivering more heat than at the rated speed then needs a specific condensing duty several times the rated one. That is the signature of the same efficiency collapse, not of a real machine. The condenser cannot carry it either: its approach temperature was sized for the requested duty, so the surplus has no driving temperature difference across the tank heat exchanger. hp_capacity is the bound because it is the only capacity the model knows the machine to have; no tolerance is added beyond floating-point noise.

On/off cycling below the minimum capacity¶

On/off cycling below the minimum continuous capacity.

A variable-speed compressor modulates down to its minimum speed and no further. A request below what the unit delivers there is met by switching the compressor on and off, and every start costs something: the refrigerant has to be redistributed and the coils brought back to their working temperatures before the capacity is there, while the compressor already draws power. EN 14825 states that loss as a degradation of the continuous-operation COP at the minimum capacity, as a function of the capacity ratio

\[CR = \frac{\text{requested heat}}{\text{capacity at the minimum step}} \qquad (0 < CR \le 1).\]

Two forms are in the standard, and both are here:

  • units delivering to air (air-to-air, water-to-air; cooling and heating) – the degradation coefficient Cd, default 0.25:

    COP_cyc = COP_min * (1 - Cd * (1 - CR))

  • units delivering to water (air-to-water, water/brine-to-water) – the degradation coefficient Cc, default 0.9:

    COP_cyc = COP_min * CR / (Cc * CR + (1 - Cc))

The ratio COP_cyc / COP_min is the part-load factor PLF. The unit delivers CR of the minimum capacity while drawing CR / PLF of the power it draws there (the power fraction).

What the two forms describe¶

  • Air. The loss is in the start: the compressor runs longer than the heat it delivers accounts for. The runtime fraction – the share of the time the compressor runs – is CR / PLF, the same number as the power fraction.

  • Water. The standard takes the start-up loss of a hydronic unit as negligible; what it charges is the power the unit keeps drawing while the compressor is off (controls, crankcase heater, circulation). Cc is one minus that off-state power over the power drawn while running, so the mean input is P_min * (CR + (1 - Cc) * (1 - CR)), which is P_min * CR / PLF. The compressor runs for CR of the time: the runtime fraction is CR, the power fraction is larger by the off-state draw. The default 0.9 is an off-state draw of a tenth of the running input; a unit with a measured standby power P_off has Cc = 1 - P_off / P_min. A request of exactly zero is the unit switched off, not on standby: no power.

The coefficients are the defaults the standard prescribes when a unit has not been tested for them; a tested value replaces them without any other change.

How a result row is averaged¶

apply_cycling() turns the continuous minimum-speed operating point into the mean over the cycling period:

  • heat and flow rates are multiplied by CR – the delivered heat equals the request, and the refrigerant cycle and both secondary streams stay in balance with it. Power that only moves the delivered heat (an indoor fan) belongs here: it runs while heat is delivered, and its heat is part of the delivery;

  • the electrical powers of the refrigeration machine (compressor, outdoor fan, source-side pump) are multiplied by the power fraction CR / PLF;

  • temperatures, pressures, speed and efficiencies keep the values of the running machine.

The electrical energy the machine draws beyond CR times its continuous power is the cycling loss; it is reported, and it is the only place where the mean row is not the running row scaled by one number. It is not part of any heat flow of the row: the refrigerant duties of the mean row are the running duties times CR, on the source side as on the delivery side.

report_cycling() is the same step for a solved row of any family (it decides whether the row cycles). The boilers use it for analyze_steady only (report_boiler_cycling(), steady_cycling()): in a dynamic run their tank thermostat already switches the compressor, which then runs at its nominal duty for part of a step, so nothing is left to average there.

Frost and defrost of an outdoor coil¶

Frost on the outdoor coil of an air-source heat pump, and its defrost.

In heating the outdoor coil runs below the outdoor air. When its surface is below 0 degC and colder than the air’s dew point, water vapour deposits on it as frost: the air passages narrow, the evaporating pressure falls, and both capacity and power drop until the unit stops heating to melt the frost. A catalogue or a certified rating at 2 degC or -7 degC is the mean over those frosting and defrosting periods, not the frost-free steady state a cycle model computes.

The correction here is the one EnergyPlus applies to its DX heating coils (Coil:Heating:DX:SingleSpeed; Engineering Reference, “Frost Adjustment Factors” and “Defrost Operation”), which it takes from DOE-2.1E (Miller and Jaster 1985). For on-demand defrost:

\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}T_{coil} &= 0.82\,T_{out} - 8.589 \qquad [^\circ C]\\\Delta\omega &= \max\left(10^{-6},\; \omega_{out} - \omega_{sat}(T_{coil})\right)\\t_{frac} &= \frac{1}{1 + 0.01446 / \Delta\omega}\\\text{heating capacity multiplier} &= 0.875\,(1 - t_{frac})\\\text{input power multiplier} &= 0.954\,(1 - t_{frac})\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

with no correction when the outdoor dry bulb is above the maximum temperature for defrost operation (5 degC). t_frac is the fraction of the compressor’s runtime spent defrosting. Reverse-cycle defrost also cools the room while it runs, an extra heating load of

\[Q_{defrost} = 0.01\,t_{frac}\,(7.222 - T_{out})\,Q_{rated} / 1.01667 .\]

Three things differ from EnergyPlus. The first is the onset of frosting:

  • Onset. In EnergyPlus the correction is a switch at the maximum outdoor temperature for defrost operation: at 5.0 degC and the default humidity the capacity multiplier is 0.771, at 5.1 degC it is 1, and the system COP of a unit steps by a quarter across a tenth of a kelvin. That limit is a setting of the defrost controller, not a temperature at which a coil frosts over: the coil-temperature correlation is at -4.5 degC there, and it puts a 0 degC coil at 10.5 degC outdoor. A coil does not frost all at once either. Near the onset its surface is close to freezing, so frost forms on part of it and for part of the time, and what a rating measures falls off gradually. Here the limit keeps its EnergyPlus meaning – no frost above T_out_max (5 degC) – and the correction grows from nothing there to its full size transition_K (3 K) below, at 2 degC: the frosting rating condition of EN 14511 (A2: 2 degC dry bulb, 1 degC wet bulb), which is also where the default humidity comes from. In between it applies to the share w of the runtime that rises linearly from zero to one:

    \[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}t_{frac} &\leftarrow w\,t_{frac}\\\text{capacity multiplier} &= 1 - w\,\left(1 - 0.875\,(1 - t_{frac})\right)\\\text{power multiplier} &= 1 - w\,\left(1 - 0.954\,(1 - t_{frac})\right)\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

    – the mean of a unit that frosts for the share w of its runtime and runs frost-free for the rest. At and below 2 degC and above 5 degC nothing differs from EnergyPlus; transition_K=0 restores the switch.

The other two are there so that no input is added:

  • Defrost power. EnergyPlus asks for a curve of defrost power per unit of rated capacity. Here the compressor is taken to draw, while it defrosts, the power it draws while it heats: E_defrost = t_frac * E_cmp.

  • Outdoor humidity. EnergyPlus reads it from the weather file. Here it is an optional input; without it the relative humidity is 84 %, the value of the EN 14511 rating condition A2 (dry bulb 2 degC, wet bulb 1 degC).

The coil temperature is the correlation above, not the evaporating temperature a cycle model solves: the constants 0.01446, 0.875 and 0.954 were fitted with that estimate, and the solved saturation temperature is a different quantity (colder than the fin surface, and dependent on load). A caller that wants its own coil temperature passes T_coil_C; the physical condition that the coil be below 0 degC is checked either way.

How a result row is averaged¶

A request Q has to be met by the mean over frosting and defrost, so the frost-free cycle is solved at the larger duty (Q + Q_defrost) / capacity multiplier. apply_defrost() then turns that frost-free row into the mean:

  • heat and flow rates are multiplied by heat_factor (delivered heat over frost-free heat), so the delivered heat is the request and the refrigerant cycle stays in balance with it. Power that only moves the delivered heat (an indoor fan) is averaged with it;

  • compressor power is multiplied by power multiplier + t_frac (heating, then defrosting) and outdoor-fan power by the power multiplier;

  • temperatures, pressures, speed and efficiencies keep the frost-free values.
